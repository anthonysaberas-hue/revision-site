# MECH 341 on one page

<div className="lede">

Two blocks. **Midterm (chapters 8–11):** Thermo 1 and exergy, gas cycles and jet engines, vapor cycles, refrigeration: every question is *states in a table, an energy balance per device, an efficiency or a COP*. **Final (chapters 12–17 plus the jet engine again):** property relations, mixtures, psychrometrics, combustion, chemical equilibrium, compressible flow: every question is *a conservation law written for a new kind of stuff* (a mixture, moist air, a reacting mixture, a moving gas). ## 1 · Which model and which table?

The first decision in every problem is **where the numbers come from**. Work down the list and stop at the first yes.

1. **Is the fluid water or R-134a and near its saturation dome (condenser, boiler, evaporator, a throttle into a mixture)?** Use the **property tables** (A-4 to A-7 for water, A-11 to A-13 for R-134a), choosing the region first: compressed liquid, saturated mixture ($v_f<v<v_g$ gives a quality $x$), superheated vapor. Interpolate $P\cdot v$ for a vapor volume, never $v$ itself (<Asg n="01">4-9</Asg>). For a compressed liquid use $h\approx h_f(T)$ plus $v\,\Delta P$ if the pressure matters (pumps).
2. **Is it a gas far from its dome (air, N₂, combustion products) at modest pressure?** It is an ideal gas. Then:
   - **Temperature range small, or no table given, or the problem says "cold-air standard":** constant $c_p$, $c_v$, $k$ (air 1.005, 0.718, 1.4; hot gas 1.147, 1.333) and $T_2/T_1=(P_2/P_1)^{(k-1)/k}$.
   - **Temperature range large (compressors with $r_p>10$, turbines at 1300 K), or "use the air table":** **air table A-17**: $h(T)$, $P_r$ and $v_r$; isentropic by $P_{r2}/P_{r1}=P_2/P_1$.
   - **Combustion products, or any gas species other than air:** the molar tables A-18 to A-26 ($\bar h$, $\bar s^\circ$ per kmol).
3. **Is the gas dense, cold or near its critical point ($T_R<2$ and $P_R>0.5$, or the question says "real gas")?** Use the **generalized charts** (the $Z$ chart A-15, departure charts A-29 for $h$ and A-30 for $s$): $\Delta\bar h=\Delta\bar h_{ideal}-R_uT_{cr}(Z_{h2}-Z_{h1})$ and the same for $s$ with $Z_s$. If the substance has tables (steam, R-134a) use the tables instead, the charts are for the rest (<Asg n="05">12-72</Asg>).
4. **Is it a mixture?** Ideal gases: Dalton for pressure, mass-weight the $c_p$ and $c_v$, mole-weight the $M$ and the molar properties. Real mixture: Kay's rule or Amagat (<Ch slug="mixtures">Chapter 13</Ch>).
5. **Moist air?** $\omega$, $\varphi$, $h=1.005T+\omega(2501+1.82T)$ and the chart (<Ch slug="psychrometrics">Chapter 14</Ch>).
6. **Reacting?** Formation enthalpies A-26 plus sensible enthalpies A-18 to A-23; $\ln K_p$ from A-28 (<Ch slug="combustion">Chapter 15</Ch>, <Ch slug="equilibrium">Chapter 16</Ch>).

**Open or closed?** Steady flow through a device (turbine, compressor, nozzle, heat exchanger, combustor with a flow): write $\dot Q-\dot W=\sum\dot m\,h_{out}-\sum\dot m\,h_{in}$ with **enthalpy** (flow work included). A fixed mass in a rigid or piston container (a tank explosion, an engine cylinder): write $Q-W=\Delta U$ with **internal energy**; in combustion that means $\bar u=\bar h-R_uT$ and the $-R_uT$ terms.

**Stagnation or static?** Use stagnation properties (subscript 0) whenever the gas **moves fast enough that the kinetic energy matters**: jet engine stations, nozzles, pitot probes, anything above about Ma 0.3 or 100 m/s. After the intake of a jet engine every station is a stagnation value; inside a nozzle you convert back to static with the Mach number. A static $T$ with a stagnation $P$ in the same equation is a bug.

**What is the question asking?**

| Wording | Move |
|---|---|
| "efficiency", "net power", "mass flow for a given power" | cycle with states 1…n, then $w$ and $q$ per kg, then $\eta$; $\dot m=\dot W/w_{net}$ |
| "COP", "tons", "power to the compressor" | refrigeration states, $\dot m=\dot Q_L/(h_1-h_4)$ |
| "dew point", "wet bulb", "coil", "make-up water" | psychrometric balances: dry air, water, energy |
| "air–fuel ratio", "percent excess air", "dew point of the products" | balance the equation, then fractions |
| "flame temperature", "heat released" | enthalpy balance of products and reactants |
| "composition at equilibrium", "extent of dissociation" | $K_p$ with $N_{total}$ and $\Delta\nu$ |
| "Mach number", "choked", "throat", "back pressure" | stagnation ratios, $A/A^*$, critical pressure ratio |

## 5 · Five reflexes

1. **Kelvin** for every ratio of temperatures, $\ln K_p$ and Clapeyron; **kmol with $R_u$, kg with $R$.**
2. **Region before table:** compressed, mixture, superheated; static or stagnation; open or closed.
3. **Sketch the T–s or $P$–$h$ diagram** with numbered states before any number.
4. **One balance per device**; at a mixing point or heat exchanger the unknown fraction comes from that balance alone (the closed heater first, then the open one).
5. **Sanity check:** an efficiency below the Carnot value, a COP in the low single digits, a quality between 0 and 1, $0<\omega$, $K_p>0$, $Ma$ consistent with the area ratio (the page of <Ch slug="gas-cycles">Chapter 9</Ch> and the "how big are the numbers" table in [the machines page](#/mech341/page/real-world) list the typical sizes).
