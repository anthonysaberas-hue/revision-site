# Combustion and flame temperature

<div className="lede">

Use this for chapter 15 when you need the balanced equation, the air-fuel ratio, the product composition, the dew point, the heat released in an open system or the adiabatic flame temperature. Pick a fuel (gas or liquid: propane, butane, octane, methanol and ethanol can be burned as liquids, with $\bar h_f$ lowered by $M h_{fg}$) or build a mixture by adding rows of mole fractions; set the percent of theoretical air, the share of carbon that goes to CO, the reactant and product temperatures and an optional heat loss. It replaces the formation enthalpies of A-26, the sensible enthalpies of A-18 to A-23 and the trial-and-error for $T_{ad}$. Dissociation is not included, so flame temperatures are the textbook values. Temperatures outside the 200 to 6000 K gas tables are flagged as "outside table range".
