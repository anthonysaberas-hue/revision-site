<Callout kind="note" title="The pattern behind all five">

(1) Fix the **four states** of the vapor-compression cycle: 1 compressor inlet (saturated vapor, or superheated if told), 2 compressor exit ($s_2=s_1$ at the condenser pressure, then the efficiency if given), 3 condenser exit (saturated liquid, or subcooled), 4 after the valve ($h_4=h_3$). (2) $q_L=h_1-h_4$, $q_H=h_2-h_3$, $w=h_2-h_1$. (3) $COP_R=q_L/w$ for a refrigerator and $COP_{HP}=q_H/w=COP_R+1$ for a heat pump. (4) With two evaporators or two stages, add **mass-flow bookkeeping** and one mixing or separator energy balance. (5) A gas refrigerator is a reversed Brayton cycle, solved with $T$ and $c_p$.

</Callout>

Find (a) the cooling rate, the power input and the COP; (b) the same parameters for the ideal vapor-compression cycle between the same pressures.

</Statement>

<Think>

The two real-life effects are separate bookkeeping lines. **Superheat** only moves state 1: $T_1=T_{sat}(200\ \text{kPa})+10.1=-10.09+10.1=0$ °C, so $h_1$ and $s_1$ come from the *superheated* table. **Subcooling** only moves state 3: $T_3=T_{sat}(1400)-4.4=52.4-4.4=48$ °C, so $h_3\approx h_f$ at 48 °C (compressed liquid, $h\approx h_f(T)$). </Think>

<Problem id="11-79" title="Gas refrigeration with a regenerator (reversed Brayton, air)" tags="gas refrigeration, regenerator, ηC, ηT, COP">

</Statement>

<Think>

A **reversed Brayton cycle**: compress, cool, expand (the turbine gives the cold), absorb heat. The regenerator cools the 35 °C air *further* (state 3 to state 4) using the cold return stream (6 to 1), so the turbine inlet is colder than 35 °C. Unknowns are found in the order: $T_2$ (compressor), $T_4$ (from the **known turbine exit** $T_5=-80$ °C and $\eta_T$), then $T_6$ from the regenerator balance, then $\varepsilon$, $\dot Q_L$ and the COP.

</Think>

<RealWorld title="Flash chambers (economizers) in industrial plants">

Screw compressors in cold stores and process chillers use an economizer port, which is this flash chamber: the vapor from the flashed liquid is injected mid-compression. It both subcools the liquid going to the evaporator (raising $q_L$) and cuts the work of the high stage.

</RealWorld>

<Traps>

- A flash chamber **inlet is only the condenser liquid** (via the valve); the quality is $x=(h_5-h_f)/h_{fg}$ at the chamber pressure.
- Flows differ by stage: $\dot m_H=\dot m_L/(1-x)$; weight the work terms accordingly.
- The mixing point uses the compressor-I discharge **and** the saturated vapor from the chamber.

</Traps>
