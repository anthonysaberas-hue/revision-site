<Callout kind="note" title="What this assignment teaches, in one paragraph">

(1) Two properties fix a moist-air state at a given pressure: $(T,\varphi)$, $(T,T_{wb})$, $(T,T_{dp})$ or $(T,\omega)$. (2) The conserved quantities in an air-conditioning device are the **mass of dry air** (so $\dot m_a$ is the same in and out), the **mass of water** ($\dot m_w=\dot m_a(\omega_1-\omega_2)$ is what condenses or is added) and **energy**. (3) Cooling and dehumidification: $\dot Q_{out}=\dot m_a(h_1-h_2)-\dot m_wh_w$ with $h_w=h_f$ at the **condensate temperature**. (4) Adiabatic mixing is a straight line on the chart, weighted by dry-air flows: $\dfrac{\dot m_{a1}}{\dot m_{a2}}=\dfrac{\omega_2-\omega_3}{\omega_3-\omega_1}=\dfrac{h_2-h_3}{h_3-h_1}$. (5) A cooling tower balances **dry air** and **water**: the air gains the evaporation, the water is cooled by the loss of $h_f$.

</Callout>

<Problem id="14-108" title="Wet cooling tower of a power plant: air flow and make-up water" tags="cooling tower, dry-air balance, water balance, energy balance">

</Statement>

<Think>

Two inlet streams (hot water 3, air 1) and two outlet streams (cooled water 4, air 2) with make-up water as the difference. $$\dot m_a=\frac{\dot m_3(h_3-h_4)}{(h_2-h_1)-(\omega_2-\omega_1)h_4}.$$

</Think>

<Problem id="14-43" title="Chart problem: 28 °C with a 20 °C dew point" tags="chart reading, dew point, vapor pressure">

<Statement>

Air at 1 atm and 28 °C has a dew point of 20 °C. Using the chart, find (a) the relative humidity, (b) the humidity ratio, (c) the enthalpy, (d) the wet-bulb temperature, (e) the vapor pressure.

<Think>

A horizontal line at the dew point fixes $\omega$ and $P_v=P_g(T_{dp})$; the intersection with the 28 °C vertical gives the state.

<Problem id="14-65" title="Cooling humid air at constant pressure to its dew point" tags="dew point, sensible cooling, 150 kPa">

<Statement>

Humid air at 150 kPa, 40 °C and 70 % relative humidity is cooled at constant pressure in a pipe to its dew-point temperature. Find the heat transfer in kJ/kg dry air. (Printed: 6.8 kJ/kg.)

</Statement>

<Think>

Cooling at constant $\omega$ ends at the dew point: $T_{dp}=T_{sat}(P_v)$. Heat is $q=h_1-h_2=c_p(T_1-T_{dp})+\omega(h_{g1}-h_{g2})$.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Vapor pressure and humidity ratio">

<T>A-4: P_g at 40 °C = 7.385 kPa</T>. $P_v=0.7(7.385)=5.170$ kPa, $\omega=\dfrac{0.622(5.170)}{150-5.170}=\mathbf{0.0222}$ (your page: 5.1696, 0.0222).

</Step>
<Step title="2. Dew point">

$T_{dp}=T_{sat}(5.170\ \mathrm{kPa})=\mathbf{33.5}$ °C (your page: 33.5 at $P_{g2}=5.1699$).

</Step>
<Step title="3. Heat transfer">

$q=c_p(T_1-T_{dp})+\omega(h_{g1}-h_{g2})=1.005(6.53)+0.0222(2573.5-2561.9)=6.56+0.26=\mathbf{6.8}$ kJ/kg dry air (from $h_1-h_2$ with $h=1.005T+\omega(2501.3+1.82T)$: 6.83).

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

**$q=6.8$ kJ/kg dry air** (printed ✔; recomputed 6.82). Your page gives the setup ($P_v$, $\omega$, $T_{dp}$ and the $h_1-h_2$ line); the final digits are not legible in the scan: **Check your page**.

</Answer>

<RealWorld title="Compressed-air dryers">

The same arithmetic sizes the aftercooler of a compressor: 150 kPa, 40 °C air with 70 % has only 6.8 kJ/kg of sensible heat above its dew point, but every further degree of cooling removes water at 2450 kJ/kg: condensation, not temperature, dominates the load.

</RealWorld>

<Traps>

- The pressure here is **150 kPa**: the dew point of the water vapor is found from $P_v$, not from $P_g(T_{dp})=P$.
- Heat is positive out; do not subtract in the wrong order.

</Traps>
