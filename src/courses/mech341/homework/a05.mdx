<Callout kind="note" title="The four tools of the chapter">

(1) **Partial derivatives and Maxwell**: $dz=M\,dx+N\,dy$ is exact if $(\partial M/\partial y)_x=(\partial N/\partial x)_y$; the four Gibbs equations ($du=T\,ds-P\,dv$, $dh=T\,ds+v\,dP$, $da=-P\,dv-s\,dT$, $dg=v\,dP-s\,dT$) give the four Maxwell relations. (2) **Clapeyron**: $\left(\dfrac{dP}{dT}\right)_{sat}=\dfrac{h_{fg}}{Tv_{fg}}$ (Clausius–Clapeyron when $v_{fg}\approx v_g=RT/P$: $\ln\dfrac{P_2}{P_1}=\dfrac{h_{fg}}{R}\left(\dfrac1{T_1}-\dfrac1{T_2}\right)$). (3) **General relations**: $du=c_vdT+\left[T\left(\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_v-P\right]dv$, $dh=c_pdT+\left[v-T\left(\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial T}\right)_P\right]dP$, $ds=\dfrac{c_p}{T}dT-\left(\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial T}\right)_PdP$. (4) **Departure charts**: $\bar h_2-\bar h_1=(\bar h_2-\bar h_1)_{ideal}-R_uT_{cr}(Z_{h2}-Z_{h1})$ and $\bar s_2-\bar s_1=(\bar s_2-\bar s_1)_{ideal}-R_u(Z_{s2}-Z_{s1})$, with $R_u$ for molar quantities and $R$ for per-kg.

</Callout>

<Problem id="12-17" title="Evaluate T, v, u, a and g from the function h = h(s, P)" tags="Gibbs equations, derivations">

<Statement>

Show how to evaluate $T$, $v$, $u$, $a$ and $g$ from the thermodynamic function $h=h(s,P)$.

</Statement>

<Think>

Compare the **total differential** of $h(s,P)$, $dh=\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right)_Pds+\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial P}\right)_sdP$, with the Gibbs relation $dh=T\,ds+v\,dP$: matching coefficients identifies $T$ and $v$; the rest follow from the definitions.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. T and v">

$T=\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right)_P$ and $v=\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial P}\right)_s$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. u, a, g from the definitions">

$u=h-Pv=\mathbf{h-P\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial P}\right)_s}$. $a=u-Ts=\mathbf{h-P\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial P}\right)_s-s\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right)_P}$. $g=h-Ts=\mathbf{h-s\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right)_P}$.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$T=(\partial h/\partial s)_P$, $v=(\partial h/\partial P)_s$, $u=h-P(\partial h/\partial P)_s$, $a=h-P(\partial h/\partial P)_s-s(\partial h/\partial s)_P$, $g=h-s(\partial h/\partial s)_P$ ✔ (your page, the December copy adds the colored corrections).

</Answer>

<RealWorld title="The Mollier diagram is this function">

$h(s,P)$ is exactly what a Mollier $h$–$s$ chart plots: the slope of an isobar is $T$, so isobars steepen with temperature; and $v=(\partial h/\partial P)_s$ is the isentropic enthalpy change per unit pressure, which is the turbine and compressor work per kg.

</RealWorld>

<Traps>

- $a$ is the Helmholtz function, $g$ the Gibbs function; the names are not interchangeable.
- Written in terms of $h(s,P)$ only: do not leave $u$ or $T$ in the answer for $a$.

</Traps>

<Problem id="12-42" title="Δu, Δh and Δs for a gas with P(v − a) = RT in an isothermal process" tags="general relations, equation of state, isothermal">

<Statement>

Derive expressions for (a) $\Delta u$, (b) $\Delta h$ and (c) $\Delta s$ for a gas whose equation of state is $P(v-a)=RT$, for an **isothermal** process. (Printed: (a) 0, (b) $a(P_2-P_1)$, (c) $-R\ln(P_2/P_1)$.)

</Statement>

<Think>

Use the general relations with the equation of state differentiated at the right variable. For $\Delta u$ the natural variables are $(T,v)$: $v=RT/P+a$, $P=RT/(v-a)$.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Δu">

$\left(\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_v=\dfrac{R}{v-a}$, so $T\left(\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_v-P=\dfrac{RT}{v-a}-P=0$. Then $du=c_vdT+0$, and for $dT=0$: $\Delta u=\mathbf0$ ✔.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Δh">

$v=\dfrac{RT}{P}+a\Rightarrow\left(\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial T}\right)_P=\dfrac{R}{P}$, so $v-T\left(\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial T}\right)_P=a$. $dh=c_pdT+a\,dP\Rightarrow\Delta h=a(P_2-P_1)$ ✔.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Δs">

$ds=\dfrac{c_p}{T}dT-\left(\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial T}\right)_PdP=\dfrac{c_p}{T}dT-\dfrac{R}{P}dP$; for $dT=0$: $\Delta s=-R\ln\dfrac{P_2}{P_1}$ ✔.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

(a) **$\Delta u=0$**, (b) **$\Delta h=a(P_2-P_1)$**, (c) **$\Delta s=-R\ln(P_2/P_1)$** ✔ (printed and your page).

</Answer>

<RealWorld title="What the constant a means">

$P(v-a)=RT$ is the ideal gas with excluded volume (a covolume $a$): molecules take up room, so $h$ depends on pressure through $a\,P$. For air at 6 bar, 20 → 300 °C with $a=0.10$ m³/kg that is a 50 kJ/kg difference: see 12-35 (the value of $a$ is exaggerated; air's real covolume is about 0.001 m³/kg).

</RealWorld>

<Traps>

- $\Delta u=0$ at constant $T$ is a *property* of this gas (its $u$ depends on $T$ only), but $\Delta h\ne0$: $h=u+Pv$ and $v$ contains $a$.
- Isothermal: drop every $dT$ term.

</Traps>
