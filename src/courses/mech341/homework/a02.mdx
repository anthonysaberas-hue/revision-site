<Callout kind="note" title="The recipe for every regenerative cycle in this assignment">

(1) Number the states on the **T–s diagram first** and mark the mass fractions ($y$ at the first heater, $z$ at the second, $1-y-z$ in the condenser). (2) One **energy balance per heater** fixes the fractions: open heater $y\,h_{bleed}+(1-y)h_{in}=h_{out}$ with $h_{out}$ a saturated liquid at the heater pressure; closed heater $y(h_{bleed}-h_{drain})=(1-y)(h_{fw,out}-h_{fw,in})$ with the feedwater leaving at the **bled steam's saturation temperature**. (3) Turbine work $=\sum$ (flow in that section) × (drop). (4) Pump work for compressed liquid: $h_{out}=h_{in}+v\,\Delta P$ (divide by $\eta_P$ if given). (5) $q_{in}=h_{boiler\ out}-h_{boiler\ in}$ (+ reheat), $\eta=1-q_{out}/q_{in}$.

</Callout>

<RealWorld title="Binary geothermal plants">

A binary (organic Rankine) plant is used when the geothermal water is too cool for steam, typically 100–180 °C: the water never flashes; it heats a low-boiling fluid such as isobutane or pentane in a heat exchanger and is reinjected. Efficiencies of **10–15 %** are typical (this problem: 13.7 %), versus the Carnot limit of about 31 % between 160 °C and 25 °C. The air-cooled condenser is used where no cooling water is available, as at many desert plants (Nevada, for example).

</RealWorld>

Steel blades at 3000–3600 rpm erode quickly when the steam is wetter than about 10 %, which is why every large steam turbine is built as HP, IP and LP sections with a reheater (hot reheat line) between them, and with 6–8 heaters.

</RealWorld>

- The bleed state 6 is *superheated* at 0.8 MPa: do not use $h_f+xh_{fg}$.
- Check the entropy you read from the table (see ⚠).

<Problem id="10-98" title="Open heater with component efficiencies, 150 MW" tags="regeneration, open feedwater heater, ηT, ηP, fraction y">

<Statement>

A steam plant of net output 150 MW operates on a regenerative Rankine cycle: steam enters the turbine at 10 MPa and 500 °C, the condenser is at 10 kPa, $\eta_T=0.80$, $\eta_P=0.95$. Steam is extracted at 0.5 MPa for an open feedwater heater whose exit is saturated liquid. Find the mass flow rate through the boiler and the thermal efficiency (and, as a follow-up in your notes, the exergy destroyed in the heater).

</Statement>

<Think>

The OFWH recipe plus *component efficiencies*: apply $\eta_T$ separately to each turbine section but always from the **same inlet state 5** ($h_{6a}=h_5-\eta_T(h_5-h_{6s})$ and $h_{7a}=h_5-\eta_T(h_5-h_{7s})$, each isentropic exit found at *its own* exit pressure), and divide each pump's work by $\eta_P$. A power requirement turns specific work into a flow: $\dot m=\dot W_{net}/w_{net}$.

</Think>
