<Callout kind="note" title="What the graded copy shows (5/6)">

<Problem id="Q2" title="Aluminum fin: heat, efficiency, effectiveness, error of the infinite-fin formula" tags="adiabatic tip, corrected length, η, ε">

<Statement>

Rectangular aluminum fins, $L=150$ mm, $w=250$ mm, $t=2$ mm, $k=177$ W/mK, base 50 °C, air 20 °C, $h=50$ W/m²K. (a) Heat lost from a fin (adiabatic tip). (b) Fin efficiency. (c) Fin effectiveness. (d) Percent error in $\dot Q$ if the infinitely long fin formula is used instead of the adiabatic tip. The sheet gives $\dot Q_{\infty}=\sqrt{hpkA_c}\theta_b$, $\dot Q_{ad}=\sqrt{hpkA_c}\theta_b\tanh(mL_c)$ and $A_{fin}=2wL_c$.

</Statement>

<Think>

Same fin as October 2021 with a part (d). The sheet's corrected length $L_c=L+t/2$ is used for the adiabatic-tip formula, so the tip's convection is included approximately.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Parameters">

$m=\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{kt}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2(50)}{177(0.002)}}=16.81$ m⁻¹ (this thin-fin form, with $p\approx2w$, is what the graded copy used; the exact $p=2(w+t)$ gives 16.87: the answers differ by 0.2 %). $L_c=0.151$ m; $mL_c=2.538$; $\tanh=0.9876$. $\sqrt{hpkA_c}=\sqrt{50(0.504)(177)(5\times10^{-4})}=1.4934$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. (a)">

$\dot Q_{ad}=1.4934(30)(0.9876)=44.25$ W.

</Step>
<Step title="3. (b) Efficiency">

$\eta=\dfrac{\dot Q_{fin}}{\dot Q_{max}}=\dfrac{\tanh(mL_c)}{mL_c}=\dfrac{0.9876}{2.538}=0.389=38.9$ %.

</Step>
<Step title="4. (c) Effectiveness">

$\varepsilon=\dfrac{\dot Q_{fin}}{hA_{c}\theta_b}=\dfrac{44.25}{50(5\times10^{-4})(30)}=59$ (the graded copy also checks it as $\eta A_{fin}/A_c=59.2$).

</Step>
<Step title="5. (d) Error">

$\dot Q_\infty=1.4934(30)=44.80$ W; error $=\dfrac{44.80-44.25}{44.80}=1.2$ % (the graded copy writes 1.23 %).

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

**(a) 44.25 W; (b) 38.9 %; (c) 59; (d) 1.2 %.**

</Answer>

<Problem id="Q3" title="A radioactive sphere cooled by convection: the full derivation" tags="sphere with generation, ODE, BCs, T(r), centre temperature, sketch">

<Statement>

A homogeneous sphere of radioactive material, $r_0=0.04$ m, generates heat at $\dot e=3\times10^7$ W/m³. The heat is dissipated by convection to $T_\infty=25$ °C with $h=4000$ W/m²K; $k=15$ W/mK. Steady, one-dimensional. (a) Write the differential equation and the boundary conditions. (b) Solve for $T(r)$. (c) Find the centre temperature. (d) Sketch the variation of heat transfer from $r=0$ to $\infty$.

</Statement>

<Think>

Spherical shell form: $\dfrac1{r^2}\dfrac d{dr}\left(r^2\dfrac{dT}{dr}\right)+\dfrac{\dot e}k=0$. Two integrations, two conditions: symmetry at $r=0$ ($dT/dr=0$) and convection at $r_0$. Do the **energy balance first**: it gives $T_s$ with no integration.

</Think>

<Plan>

(a) ODE + BCs $\to$ (b) integrate $\to$ $T_s$ from the energy balance $\to$ $T(r)$ $\to$ (c) $T_0$ $\to$ (d) $\dot Q(r)=\dot e\frac43\pi r^3$ for $r<r_0$, constant beyond.

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. (a) Equation and boundary conditions">

$\dfrac1{r^2}\dfrac d{dr}\left(r^2\dfrac{dT}{dr}\right)+\dfrac{\dot e}k=0$. BC 1 (symmetry): $\dfrac{dT}{dr}\Big\vert_{r=0}=0$. BC 2 (convection at the surface): $-k\dfrac{dT}{dr}\Big\vert_{r_0}=h[T(r_0)-T_\infty]$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. (b) Integrate">

$r^2\dfrac{dT}{dr}=-\dfrac{\dot er^3}{3k}+C_1\Rightarrow\dfrac{dT}{dr}=-\dfrac{\dot er}{3k}+\dfrac{C_1}{r^2}$; finite flux at the centre requires $C_1=0$ (this is BC 1). $T(r)=-\dfrac{\dot er^2}{6k}+C_2$.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Surface temperature by an energy balance">

All the generated heat leaves through the surface: $\dot e\tfrac43\pi r_0^3=h(4\pi r_0^2)(T_s-T_\infty)\Rightarrow T_s=T_\infty+\dfrac{\dot er_0}{3h}=25+\dfrac{3\times10^7(0.04)}{3(4000)}=125$ °C.

</Step>
<Step title="4. $T(r)$">

$C_2=T_s+\dfrac{\dot er_0^2}{6k}$, so $T(r)=T_s+\dfrac{\dot e}{6k}(r_0^2-r^2)=125+\dfrac{3\times10^7}{90}(0.0016-r^2)=125+3.333\times10^5(0.0016-r^2)$ (°C, $r$ in m).

</Step>
<Step title="5. (c) Centre">

$T_0=T_s+\dfrac{\dot er_0^2}{6k}=125+\dfrac{3\times10^7(0.0016)}{6(15)}=125+533.3=658.3$ °C.

</Step>
<Step title="6. (d) Sketch">

Heat **rate** through a sphere of radius $r$: $\dot Q(r)=\dot e\tfrac43\pi r^3$ for $r\le r_0$ (cubic, up to $\dot Q=3\times10^7(2.681\times10^{-4})=8040$ W at $r_0$) and **constant $=8040$ W for $r>r_0$** (no generation outside). If the heat **flux** is sketched: $q''=\dot er/3$ (linear in $r$) inside, up to $\dot er_0/3=4\times10^5$ W/m² at $r_0$, then $q''=\dfrac{8040}{4\pi r^2}$ falling as $1/r^2$.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

**$T_s=125$ °C; $T(r)=125+3.33\times10^5(0.0016-r^2)$ °C; $T_0=658.3$ °C; $\dot Q(r)\propto r^3$ up to 8.04 kW at $r_0$, then constant.**

</Answer>

<Callout kind="note" title="On the graded copy (8/8 marks)">

The student carried $C_1$ and $C_2$ through a long expression using BC 2 first, then set $C_1=0$ at the end (which the grader flagged on the page with "$C_1=0$"); a shorter route is to impose symmetry first ($C_1=0$), then use the energy balance for $T_s$. The final answers $T_s=125$ °C and $T_0=658.33$ °C are right. In part (d) the plotted curve (heat transfer rate versus $r$) should rise as $r^3$ up to $r_0$ and then be **flat**; any curve that keeps increasing after $r_0$ would be wrong.

</Callout>

<Traps>

- The $\dfrac{\dot e r^2}{6k}$ coefficient is for a **sphere** (cylinder 4, plane wall 2).
- $T_s$ comes from the energy balance on the whole body, not from $T_\infty$ and $k$ alone.
- A "flux" sketch is linear then $1/r^2$; a "rate" sketch is cubic then flat.

</Traps>
