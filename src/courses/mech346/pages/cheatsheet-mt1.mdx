## 1 · The three laws and the energy balance

- Fourier: $\dot Q_{cond}=-kA\dfrac{dT}{dx}$. Newton: $\dot Q_{conv}=hA_s(T_s-T_\infty)$. Stefan–Boltzmann: $\dot Q_{rad}=\varepsilon\sigma A_s(T_s^4-T_{surr}^4)$, **kelvin**.
- Steady, no generation: heat in = heat out. Generation: $\dot E_{gen}=\dot eV$. Transient: $\dot E_{in}-\dot E_{out}+\dot E_{gen}=\dfrac{dE}{dt}$.
- Radiation as a convection-like term: $h_{rad}=\varepsilon\sigma(T_s^2+T_{surr}^2)(T_s+T_{surr})$ (about 5–6 W/m²K near room temperature, $\varepsilon\approx0.9$); parallel to convection: add the coefficients.
- Radiation needs **no medium**. Convection depends on the **fluid**, not the solid's $k$. Contact resistance: roughness, materials, interface pressure and temperature, trapped fluid (not convection).

## 2 · The conduction equation (Ch 2)

- General: $\nabla\cdot(k\nabla T)+\dot e=\rho c_p\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial t}$. Constant $k$: $\nabla^2T+\dfrac{\dot e}k=\dfrac1\alpha\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial t}$, $\alpha=\dfrac k{\rho c_p}$.
- 1-D steady: plane wall $\dfrac{d^2T}{dx^2}+\dfrac{\dot e}k=0$; cylinder $\dfrac1r\dfrac d{dr}\left(r\dfrac{dT}{dr}\right)+\dfrac{\dot e}k=0$; sphere $\dfrac1{r^2}\dfrac d{dr}\left(r^2\dfrac{dT}{dr}\right)+\dfrac{\dot e}k=0$.
- **Boundary conditions:** fixed $T$; fixed flux $-k\,dT/dx=q''$; **insulated/symmetric** $dT/dx=0$; **convection** $-k\dfrac{dT}{dx}\Big\vert_s=h(T_s-T_\infty)$; radiation.
- **Generation, centre-to-surface rise** (uniform $\dot e$): plane wall (half-thickness $L$) $\dfrac{\dot eL^2}{2k}$; long cylinder $\dfrac{\dot er_0^2}{4k}$; sphere $\dfrac{\dot er_0^2}{6k}$.
- **Surface temperature by energy balance:** $T_s=T_\infty+\dfrac{\dot eV}{hA_s}$ = $T_\infty+\dfrac{\dot eL}h$ (wall), $+\dfrac{\dot er_0}{2h}$ (cylinder), $+\dfrac{\dot er_0}{3h}$ (sphere).
- **Method for Q3:** ODE → integrate twice → two BCs ($C_1=0$ from symmetry; convection or fixed $T$ at the surface) → $T(r)$ → centre → heat rate $=\dot eV$ (inside) and constant outside. Sphere $T(r)=T_s+\dfrac{\dot e(r_0^2-r^2)}{6k}$.
- Heat **flux** sketch: wall $q''=\dot ex$ (linear), cylinder $\dot er/2$, sphere $\dot er/3$ inside; outside no generation: $q''\propto1/r$ (cylinder), $1/r^2$ (sphere). The heat **rate** $\dot Q(r)\propto r^3$ (sphere) up to $r_0$, then constant.
- Variable $k=k_0(1+\beta T)$: use $k$ at the mean temperature.

## 3 · Resistance networks (Ch 3)

| Element | $R$ |
|---|---|
| plane wall | $\dfrac L{kA}$ |
| cylinder | $\dfrac{\ln(r_2/r_1)}{2\pi kL}$ |
| sphere | $\dfrac{r_2-r_1}{4\pi kr_1r_2}$ |
| convection | $\dfrac1{hA}$ |
| radiation | $\dfrac1{h_{rad}A}$ |
| contact | $\dfrac{R''_c}A=\dfrac1{h_cA}$ |

Series: add. Parallel: $\dfrac1R=\sum\dfrac1{R_i}$. $\dot Q=\dfrac{\Delta T_{total}}{R_{tot}}$; interface temperature $T_i=T_{start}\mp\dot Q\,R_{partial}$. **Draw the circuit first.** Composite wall with layers and parallel paths (studs): series–parallel, 1-D approximation.

- **Critical radius** of insulation (cylinder) $r_{cr}=k_{ins}/h_o$; sphere $2k_{ins}/h_o$. If $r_{outer}<r_{cr}$, insulation **increases** the loss; the maximum loss is at $r_{cr}$.
- Large $T$-drop $\Leftrightarrow$ large $R$: the layer with the largest resistance takes the largest share of $\Delta T$.

## 4 · Fins (adiabatic tip unless stated)

- $m=\sqrt{\dfrac{hp}{kA_c}}$ (thin rectangular fin: $\sqrt{2h/kt}$; pin: $\sqrt{4h/kD}$). $p$=perimeter, $A_c$=cross-section.
- $\theta(x)/\theta_b=\dfrac{\cosh m(L-x)}{\cosh mL}$; **tip** $=1/\cosh mL$.
- $\dot Q_{ad}=\sqrt{hpkA_c}\,\theta_b\tanh(mL)$; $\dot Q_\infty=\sqrt{hpkA_c}\,\theta_b$ (need $mL\gtrsim3$); convective tip: use the **corrected length** $L_c=L+\dfrac{A_c}p$ ($t/2$ for a plate, $D/4$ for a pin).
- **Efficiency** $\eta=\dfrac{\dot Q_{fin}}{hA_{fin}\theta_b}=\dfrac{\tanh(mL_c)}{mL_c}$; **effectiveness** $\varepsilon=\dfrac{\dot Q_{fin}}{hA_c\theta_b}=\eta\dfrac{A_{fin}}{A_c}$; worth it if $\varepsilon>2$.
- Array: $\dot Q=\eta h A_{fin}\theta_b+hA_{unfinned}\theta_b$. Error of the infinite-fin formula $=\dfrac1{\tanh mL}-1$.
- Fins help most when $h$ is **small** (gas side) and $k$ is large.
