<Problem id="4-16" title="800 W iron: how long to reach 140 °C? (lumped with heat input)" tags="lumped system with a heat source, steady-state temperature rise">

<Statement>

The base plate of an 800 W iron is 0.5 cm thick aluminum alloy 2024-T6 ($\rho=2770$, $c_p=875$ J/kgK, $\alpha=7.3\times10^{-5}$ m²/s), area $0.03$ m², initially at the ambient 22 °C, $h=12$ W/m²K; 85 % of the heater power goes to the plate. How long does the plate take to reach 140 °C? Is it realistic to assume the plate is uniform at all times?

</Statement>

<Think>

Heating **with a source**: $\rho Vc_p\dfrac{dT}{dt}=\dot Q_{in}-hA(T-T_\infty)$, so $T-T_\infty=\dfrac{\dot Q_{in}}{hA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)$ with $\tau=\dfrac{\rho c_pL}h$. First check $Bi$ with $k=\alpha\rho c_p$.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="Your steps">

$k=\rho\alpha c_p=177$ W/mK; $Bi=\dfrac{hL_c}k=3.4\times10^{-4}\ll0.1$ (your $L_c=V/A=0.005$). Your first attempt used the **cooling** form $e^{-bt}$ (and solved for $T_i$, getting $t=2812$ s); you then switched to the heating form $\dfrac{T-T_\infty}{T_i-T_\infty}=1-e^{-bt}$ with $b=9.9\times10^{-4}$ s⁻¹, $0.06247=1-e^{-9.9\times10^{-4}t}$.

</Step>
<Step title="Recomputation">

$\dot Q_{in}=0.85(800)=680$ W; $\dfrac{\dot Q_{in}}{hA}=1888.9$ K (the plate would end 1889 K above ambient); $\tau=\dfrac{2770(875)(0.005)}{12}=1010$ s. $\dfrac{118}{1888.9}=0.06247=1-e^{-t/\tau}\Rightarrow t=-1010\ln(0.93753)=65.1$ s.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

**$t\approx65$ s**; yes, uniform plate is realistic ($Bi=3.4\times10^{-4}$).

</Answer>

<Callout kind="note" title="✔ Setup matches; the last digits you wrote read 65.7 vs my 65.1. What it teaches">

Know which side of the equation the source is on: with heat **input** the exponential multiplies $(\dot Q/hA)$ and the temperature *rises toward* $T_\infty+\dot Q/hA$; with no source it decays to $T_\infty$. Your discarded first attempt (2812 s) treated the iron as a cooling body, which is why it failed. Because $t\ll\tau$, $T\approx T_\infty+\dot Q t/(\rho Vc_p)$: the plate heats almost adiabatically.

</Callout>

$A=0.02823$ m², $\tau=7009$ s, $t=7009\ln\dfrac{18.5}{4.5}=9908$ s $=2.75$ h; $Bi=7.5(0.01257)/0.595=0.159$.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

**About 2 h 45 min** (lumped), with $Bi\approx0.16>0.1$: only an estimate.

</Answer>

<Problem id="P4-1" title="Thermocouple response time" tags="lumped capacitance, time constant, Bi check">

<Statement>

A spherical thermocouple junction of diameter 1 mm ($k=35$ W/mK, $\rho=8500$ kg/m³, $c_p=320$ J/kgK) is placed in a gas stream with $h=200$ W/m²K. Find the time constant and how long it takes to indicate 99 % of a sudden change in gas temperature.

</Statement>

<Think>

Tiny, conductive bead: check $Bi=hL_c/k$ with $L_c=V/A_s=D/6$. If $Bi<0.1$ the temperature is uniform and $\dfrac{T-T_\infty}{T_i-T_\infty}=e^{-t/\tau}$ with $\tau=\dfrac{\rho Vc_p}{hA_s}=\dfrac{\rho c_pL_c}h$. "99 % of the change" means $\theta/\theta_i=0.01$.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Biot number">

$L_c=D/6=1.667\times10^{-4}$ m; $Bi=\dfrac{200(1.667\times10^{-4})}{35}=9.5\times10^{-4}\ll0.1$: lumped is valid.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Time constant">

$\tau=\dfrac{\rho c_pL_c}h=\dfrac{8500(320)(1.667\times10^{-4})}{200}=2.27$ s.

</Step>
<Step title="3. 99 % response">

$e^{-t/\tau}=0.01\Rightarrow t=\tau\ln100=4.605(2.27)=10.4$ s.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

**$\tau=2.27$ s; 99 % response in 10.4 s.** To make a faster sensor: smaller bead (smaller $L_c$), higher $h$ (faster flow), lower $\rho c_p$.

</Answer>

</Problem>

<Problem id="P4-2" title="Quenching a steel sphere (one-term solution)" tags="Bi greater than 0.1, sphere, center time, Q">

<Statement>

A steel sphere ($r_0=5$ cm, $k=40$ W/mK, $\rho=7800$ kg/m³, $c_p=500$ J/kgK, $\alpha=1.026\times10^{-5}$ m²/s) at 500 °C is quenched in a 30 °C bath with $h=1000$ W/m²K. Find (a) the time for the centre to reach 100 °C, (b) the surface temperature then, (c) the heat removed by then.

</Statement>

<Think>

First **check $Bi$**: with $L_c=r_0/3$, $Bi=\dfrac{1000(0.01667)}{40}=0.417>0.1$, so **lumped fails**; use the one-term solution with $Bi=hr_0/k=1.25$ (the exam formula sheet uses the radius). Roots: $1-\lambda_1\cot\lambda_1=Bi$, $A_1=\dfrac{4(\sin\lambda_1-\lambda_1\cos\lambda_1)}{2\lambda_1-\sin2\lambda_1}$.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Eigenvalue and coefficient for $Bi=1.25$">

$\lambda_1=1.7155$, $A_1=1.3313$ (Table 4-2 gives $Bi=1.0$: $1.5708,\ 1.2732$ and $Bi=2.0$: $2.0288,\ 1.4793$; interpolation gives roughly 1.70 and 1.33 – the exact roots above are best).

</Step>
<Step title="2. Centre time">

$\theta_0=\dfrac{100-30}{500-30}=0.1489=A_1e^{-\lambda_1^2Fo}\Rightarrow Fo=-\dfrac{\ln(0.1489/1.3313)}{1.7155^2}=0.7443$. Check $Fo>0.2$ ✓. $t=\dfrac{Fo\,r_0^2}\alpha=\dfrac{0.7443(0.0025)}{1.026\times10^{-5}}=181$ s.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Surface temperature">

$\dfrac{T_s-T_\infty}{T_i-T_\infty}=\theta_0\dfrac{\sin\lambda_1}{\lambda_1}=0.1489(0.5768)=0.0859$, so $T_s=30+470(0.0859)=70.4$ °C (the surface is 30 K colder than the centre).

</Step>
<Step title="4. Heat removed">

$Q_{max}=\rho Vc_p(T_i-T_\infty)=7800\left(\tfrac43\pi(0.05)^3\right)(500)(470)=9.60\times10^5$ J. $\dfrac Q{Q_{max}}=1-3\theta_0\dfrac{\sin\lambda_1-\lambda_1\cos\lambda_1}{\lambda_1^3}=0.8905$, so $Q=855$ kJ.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

**(a) 181 s; (b) 70.4 °C; (c) 855 kJ (89 % of the maximum).**

</Answer>

<Traps>

Using the lumped formula here would predict the whole body at 100 °C already after $\tau\ln(470/70)$ with $\tau=\rho c_pL_c/h=65$ s: 124 s, an under-estimate of the centre time by one third.

</Traps>

</Problem>

<Problem id="P4-3" title="Freezing of soil and touching hot things (semi-infinite solid)" tags="erf, penetration depth, contact temperature">

<Statement>

(a) Soil ($\alpha=1.38\times10^{-7}$ m²/s) is initially at 10 °C. Its surface is suddenly held at $-10$ °C for 60 days. Find the temperature at depths 0.5 m and 1 m, and the depth where the soil is at 0 °C. (b) A finger (skin: $k=0.37$, $\rho=1000$, $c_p=3400$, at 34 °C) touches a 20 °C aluminum block ($k=237$, $\rho=2702$, $c_p=903$) and a 20 °C wooden block ($k=0.17$, $\rho=380$, $c_p=1260$). Find the contact temperatures.

</Statement>

<Think>

(a) Semi-infinite solid with a fixed surface temperature: $\dfrac{T-T_i}{T_s-T_i}=\mathrm{erfc}\left(\dfrac x{2\sqrt{\alpha t}}\right)$. (b) Two semi-infinite solids brought into contact: $T_c=\dfrac{b_1T_1+b_2T_2}{b_1+b_2}$ with the effusivity $b=\sqrt{k\rho c_p}$.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. (a) Time scale">

$t=60(86\,400)=5.184\times10^6$ s; $\sqrt{\alpha t}=\sqrt{0.7154}=0.846$ m; $2\sqrt{\alpha t}=1.692$ m (the "penetration depth" ≈ 1.7 m).

</Step>
<Step title="2. (a) Temperatures">

$T(x)=-10+20\,\mathrm{erf}\left(\dfrac x{1.692}\right)$. At $x=0.5$: $\mathrm{erf}(0.2955)=0.3240$, $T=-3.52$ °C. At $x=1$: $\mathrm{erf}(0.591)=0.5967$, $T=1.93$ °C.

</Step>
<Step title="3. (a) Frost depth">

$T=0\Rightarrow\mathrm{erf}(\eta)=0.5\Rightarrow\eta=0.4769$, so $x=0.4769(1.692)=0.807$ m: bury water pipes deeper than about 0.8 m.

</Step>
<Step title="4. (b) Effusivities">

$b_{skin}=\sqrt{0.37(1000)(3400)}=1122$; $b_{Al}=24\,047$; $b_{wood}=285$ (all in W·s$^{1/2}$/m²K).

</Step>
<Step title="5. (b) Contact temperatures">

Aluminum: $T_c=\dfrac{1122(34)+24\,047(20)}{1122+24\,047}=20.6$ °C. Wood: $T_c=\dfrac{1122(34)+285(20)}{1122+285}=31.2$ °C.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

**(a) $-3.5$ °C at 0.5 m, 1.9 °C at 1 m, frost depth 0.81 m; (b) 20.6 °C (aluminum feels cold) and 31.2 °C (wood feels warm).** The finger perceives the *contact temperature*, not the block temperature or its conductivity alone.

</Answer>

</Problem>

<Problem id="P4-4" title="Heating a short stainless-steel cylinder (product solution)" tags="product solution, short cylinder, Bi check, lumped fails">

<Statement>

A stainless-steel cylinder ($D=10$ cm, $L=10$ cm, $k=15$ W/mK, $\rho=8000$ kg/m³, $c_p=480$ J/kgK, $\alpha=3.906\times10^{-6}$ m²/s) at 250 °C is placed in 25 °C air with $h=300$ W/m²K on all surfaces. Find the time for the **centre** to cool to 100 °C and the heat removed by then.

</Statement>

<Think>

A finite cylinder is the **intersection** of an infinite cylinder (radius $r_0=5$ cm) and an infinite plane wall of half-thickness $L_h=5$ cm. $\theta_{short}=\theta_{cyl}\cdot\theta_{wall}$. First the Biot check: $L_c=V/A_s=1.667$ cm, $Bi=0.33>0.1$, so lumped is not allowed. The Biot numbers for the two factors are both $hr_0/k=hL_h/k=1.0$.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Coefficients from Table 4-2 for $Bi=1.0$">

Infinite cylinder: $\lambda_1=1.2558$, $A_1=1.2071$. Plane wall: $\lambda_1=0.8603$, $A_1=1.1191$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Centre temperature as a function of time">

$\theta_0=\dfrac{T_0-T_\infty}{T_i-T_\infty}=\left[A_{1c}e^{-\lambda_{1c}^2Fo_c}\right]\left[A_{1w}e^{-\lambda_{1w}^2Fo_w}\right]$. Both Fourier numbers are equal because $r_0=L_h$: $Fo=\dfrac{\alpha t}{(0.05)^2}$. Requirement: $\theta_0=\dfrac{100-25}{250-25}=0.3333$.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Solve for $Fo$">

$0.3333=1.2071(1.1191)\exp[-(1.5770+0.7401)Fo]=1.3509e^{-2.3171Fo}\Rightarrow Fo=\dfrac{\ln(1.3509/0.3333)}{2.3171}=0.604$. Check $Fo>0.2$ ✓ for both factors.

</Step>
<Step title="4. Time">

$t=\dfrac{Fo\,r_0^2}\alpha=\dfrac{0.604(0.0025)}{3.906\times10^{-6}}=387$ s (6.4 min).

</Step>
<Step title="5. Heat removed (product formula for $Q$)">

Centre values: $\theta_{0,c}=1.2071e^{-1.577(0.604)}=0.4657$, $\theta_{0,w}=1.1191e^{-0.7401(0.604)}=0.7157$. $\dfrac Q{Q_{max}}\Big\vert_{wall}=1-\theta_{0,w}\dfrac{\sin\lambda_1}{\lambda_1}=0.3694$; $\Big\vert_{cyl}=1-2\theta_{0,c}\dfrac{J_1(\lambda_1)}{\lambda_1}=0.6202$. Combined: $0.3694+0.6202(1-0.3694)=0.7605$. $Q_{max}=\rho Vc_p\Delta T=8000(7.854\times10^{-4})(480)(225)=6.79\times10^5$ J, so $Q=516$ kJ.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

**Centre reaches 100 °C after 387 s; $Q\approx516$ kJ (76 % of the maximum).**

</Answer>

<Traps>

- The lumped formula would give $\tau=\rho Vc_p/hA_s=213$ s and $t=234$ s: **40 % too short**, because the centre of a body with $Bi=0.33$ lags the surface.
- $Q_{max}$ must use the **actual** volume of the finite cylinder, not the infinite-body volume.
- Both Fourier numbers use their **own** length: here they happen to coincide.

</Traps>

</Problem>
