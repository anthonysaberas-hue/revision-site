## The one-sentence course

> **Model a system as a transfer function (or state model), look at its poles, and use feedback to move the poles where you want them.**

Everything in the course is one of those three verbs: *model* (Block 1 and 2), *look at the poles* (Block 1 and 3: time response, stability), *move them* (Block 3: PID, root locus, Bode, compensators).

## Block 1 · Mathematics and mechanics (MT1, Lectures 1–8)

| Idea | The thing to remember | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Feedback loop | measure, compare, correct; disturbance and noise enter the loop | <Ch slug="intro">1</Ch> |
| Laplace | calculus becomes algebra; $\mathcal L\{\dot f\}=sF-f(0)$; poles set the *shape*, residues the *amount*; check with IVT/FVT | <Ch slug="laplace">2</Ch> |
| Mechanical models | Newton / energy; $\omega_n=\sqrt{k/m}$, $\zeta=\dfrac{c}{2\sqrt{km}}$; massless node = forces balance | <Ch slug="mech-models">3</Ch> |
| Time response | $OS=100e^{-\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}$, $t_p=\pi/\omega_d$, $t_s=4/(\zeta\omega_n)$; a nearby zero raises overshoot | <Ch slug="time-response">4</Ch> |
| Block diagrams | $\dfrac{G}{1+GH}$; Mason $\sum P_k\Delta_k/\Delta$ | <Ch slug="block-diagrams">5</Ch> |
| Frequency response | $s=j\omega$; $|G|$ and $\angle G$; corners, slopes | <Ch slug="frequency-basics">6</Ch> |
| State space | $\dot x=Ax+Bu$; $G=C(sI-A)^{-1}B+D$; $\Phi=(sI-A)^{-1}$; Method 1/2; eigenvalues = poles | <Ch slug="state-space">7</Ch> |

**Poles in one picture.** Left half-plane: decays; imaginary axis: sustained oscillation; right half-plane: grows. Real part sets the decay rate $\sigma$, imaginary part the oscillation $\omega_d$, the angle from the negative real axis $\cos^{-1}\zeta$.

## Block 2 · Physical systems as circuits (MT2, Lectures 9–16)

The trick of the whole block: **every physical system has potential and flux, so every system is a circuit.**

| System | Potential | Flux | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| electrical | voltage | current | <Ch slug="circuits-opamps">8</Ch> |
| mechanical (mobility) | velocity | force | <Ch slug="analogies">10</Ch> |
| mechanical (impedance) | force | velocity | <Ch slug="analogies">10</Ch> |
| acoustic / hydraulic / pneumatic | pressure | volume (mass) flow | <Ch slug="acoustic-pneumatic">12</Ch>, <Ch slug="hydraulic-thermal">13</Ch> |
| liquid level | head | volume flow | <Ch slug="hydraulic-thermal">13</Ch> |
| thermal | temperature | heat flow | <Ch slug="hydraulic-thermal">13</Ch> |

- **Circuits and op-amps:** $Z_R=R$, $Z_C=\tfrac1{Cs}$, $Z_L=Ls$; inverting op-amp $-Z_2/Z_1$; always take $i$ at nodes.
- **Linearisation:** operating point (derivatives zero), Taylor expand, incremental resistance $=1/(di/de)$; never use Laplace before linearising. <Ch slug="linearization">Chapter 9</Ch>
- **Analogies:** force source first; masses to ground in mobility; transformer for the same analog, gyrator for different analogs; lever = transformer. <Ch slug="analogies">Chapter 10</Ch>
- **DC motor:** $\dfrac\Omega V=\dfrac{K_t}{(L_as+R_a)(Js+B)+K_bK_t}$ (**plus**); back emf is extra damping. <Ch slug="electromechanical">Chapter 11</Ch>
- **Acoustic / pneumatic:** cavity = shunt capacitor, neck = series inductor; nozzle–flapper; pneumatic PID from two RC lags. <Ch slug="acoustic-pneumatic">Chapter 12</Ch>
- **Hydraulic / thermal:** tank = capacitor, valve = resistor $2H/Q$; dashpot $T=\rho RA^2/k$; servo = integrator. <Ch slug="hydraulic-thermal">Chapter 13</Ch>
- **Sensitivity:** $S^T_K=S^T_GS^G_K$, $S=\tfrac1{1+L}$: feedback buys low sensitivity at low frequency. <Ch slug="sensitivity">Chapter 14</Ch>

## Block 3 · Feedback design (Final, Lectures 17–23)

| Tool | Question it answers | Key formula | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Error constants | how accurate? | $K_p,K_v,K_a$; type decides | <Ch slug="steady-state-error">15</Ch> |
| Routh | stable? for which $K$? | first-column sign changes | <Ch slug="routh">16</Ch> |
| PID / Z–N | quick tuning | $K_p=0.6K_{cr}$, $T_i=0.5P_{cr}$, $T_d=0.125P_{cr}$ | <Ch slug="pid">17</Ch> |
| Root locus | where do the poles go as $K$ grows? | 10 rules, $|KG|=1$ | <Ch slug="root-locus">18</Ch> |
| Bode and margins | how far from instability? | $PM$ at gain crossover, $GM$ at phase crossover | <Ch slug="bode-margins">19</Ch> |
| Lead / lag | fix transient / fix accuracy | $\alpha=\tfrac{1-\sin\varphi_m}{1+\sin\varphi_m}$; $K_c\beta=1$ | <Ch slug="lead-lag">20</Ch> |
| Nyquist | stable by the curve | $Z=N+P$ | <Ch slug="nyquist">21</Ch> |

**The Block 3 story in four lines.** Accuracy wants high gain and integrators; stability wants low gain. A gain alone cannot have both ($K/[s(s+1)(s+5)]$: $K=25$ gives $e_{ss}=0.2$ but $PM=4^\circ$). A **lag** buys accuracy at low frequency; a **lead** buys phase near crossover; PID does both. The root locus, Bode plot and Nyquist curve are three views of the same fact.

## The ten things never to forget

1. Poles decide everything about the shape; check the signs of their real parts first.
2. Always check a transform or a model with its limits: $t=0$ (IVT), $t=\infty$ (FVT, only if stable), DC gain, high-frequency gain, a special case you know.
3. State space: states are energy variables; convert back to $G(s)$ to verify.
4. A massless node: forces sum to zero.
5. Drawing an analog: force source first; nodes are velocities; same analog = transformer, different = gyrator.
6. Nonlinear circuit: operating point, then Taylor, then (and only then) Laplace; incremental resistance is a slope.
7. The back emf adds damping: $+K_bK_t$.
8. Final-value theorem and Z–N need a stable closed loop / are first cuts, not designs.
9. Always **verify** a compensator by recomputing the margins.
10. **The official solutions contain errors**: recompute (see the errata page).
