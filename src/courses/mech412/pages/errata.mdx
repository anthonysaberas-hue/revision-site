# Errata: mistakes in the official solutions, the slides and Anthony's notes

<div className="lede">

This course was one of the most disorganised and the official material is full of errors. **Everything on this page was recomputed** (numpy, scipy, sympy, the course library) and the corrected version is what the rest of this site teaches. Legend: <strong>✘ definite error</strong> (recomputed), <strong>⚠ convention-dependent</strong>. Each problem page also shows its own errors inline as a "⚠ Official solution error" box. If you ever see a formula in this course that surprises you, **recompute before you trust it**.

</div>

<Callout kind="warn" title="The five that would cost the most marks">

1. **HW7 Q3**: the official solution says the acceleration error of the PI design is **0**; it is $375/K_I=2.18$ (<Hw n="7">HW7</Hw>).
2. **HW10 Q3** (Dorf AP10.9): the printed lead-lag compensator is **missing a gain of 50**: as printed $K_v=2$, not 100 (<Hw n="10">HW10</Hw>).
3. **T07 Q1** and Anthony's crib: the DC-motor denominator has **$+K_bK_t$**, not a minus (<Ch slug="electromechanical">Chapter 11</Ch>).
4. **MT1 W2019 Q4**: official undershoot 39 % is **40.7 %**, and "no way to reduce it" is false (lower $R_2/R_1$).
5. **Final F2010 P7(c)**: the requested gain $K=5/3$ makes the loop **unstable**; there is no valid answer.

</Callout>

<Callout kind="note" title="How to use this page in the exam">

When an official solution disagrees with you, check three things in this order: **signs** (back emf, feedback, disturbance), **units** (rad/s vs Hz, mass flow vs volume flow), **limits** (DC value, high-frequency value, a special case that must reduce to a known result). Almost every error above fails one of these.

</Callout>
