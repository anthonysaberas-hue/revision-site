<Problem id="MT1 F2007 Q1" title="Response to a rectangular pulse (8 marks)" tags="time shift, repeated pole, partial fractions">

<Statement>

$\dfrac{X(s)}{U(s)}=\dfrac{1}{s(s^2+s)}=\dfrac{1}{s^2(s+1)}$. The input is a pulse of height 1 between $t=0.5$ s and $t=1$ s (zero otherwise); all initial conditions zero. Find $x(t)$ for $t>1$ s.

</Statement>

<Think>

A pulse is "switch on at 0.5, switch off at 1": $u=1(t-0.5)-1(t-1)$. By linearity the response is the response to a step starting at 0.5 **minus** the response to a step starting at 1. For $t>1$ both parts are active, so just add them with their time shifts.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Input as two steps">

$u(t)=1(t-0.5)-1(t-1)$. With the time-shift property ($e^{-as}/s$): $U(s)=\dfrac{e^{-0.5s}-e^{-s}}{s}$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Output">

$X(s)=\dfrac{U(s)}{s^2(s+1)}=\dfrac{e^{-0.5s}-e^{-s}}{s^3(s+1)}$.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Partial fractions of H(s) = 1/[s³(s+1)]">

Triple pole at 0 and a simple pole at $-1$: $\dfrac1{s^3(s+1)}=\dfrac A{s+1}+\dfrac B{s^3}+\dfrac C{s^2}+\dfrac Ds$.

- $A$ (cover-up at $s=-1$): $\dfrac1{s^3}\Big|_{-1}=-1$.
- $B$ (multiply by $s^3$, set $s=0$): $\dfrac1{s+1}\Big|_0=1$.
- $C$ (first derivative of $\tfrac1{s+1}$ at 0): $-\dfrac1{(s+1)^2}=-1$.
- $D$ (half the second derivative): $\tfrac12\cdot\dfrac2{(s+1)^3}=1$.

So $\dfrac1{s^3(s+1)}=-\dfrac1{s+1}+\dfrac1{s^3}-\dfrac1{s^2}+\dfrac1s$. **Check at $s=1$:** LHS $=\tfrac12$; RHS $=-\tfrac12+1-1+1=\tfrac12$ ✔.

</Step>
<Step title="4. Invert H">

$h(t)=-e^{-t}+\dfrac{t^2}2-t+1$ ($t\ge0$). By the shift theorem the pulse response is $x(t)=h(t-0.5)1(t-0.5)-h(t-1)1(t-1)$.

</Step>
<Step title="5. For t > 1 both steps are on">

$x=\big[-e^{-(t-0.5)}+\tfrac{(t-0.5)^2}2-(t-0.5)+1\big]-\big[-e^{-(t-1)}+\tfrac{(t-1)^2}2-(t-1)+1\big]$. The linear terms combine: $-(t-0.5)+(t-1)=-0.5$, so
$$x(t)=e^{-(t-1)}-e^{-(t-0.5)}+\tfrac12\big[(t-0.5)^2-(t-1)^2\big]-0.5 .$$

</Step>
<Step title="6. Simplify">

$\tfrac12[(t-0.5)^2-(t-1)^2]=\tfrac12(t-0.75)=0.5t-0.375$ and $e^{1-t}-e^{0.5-t}=e^{1-t}(1-e^{-0.5})$ with $1-e^{-0.5}=0.3935$:
$$x(t)=0.3935\,e^{1-t}+0.5t-0.875\qquad(t>1).$$

</Step>
<Step title="7. Checks">

(i) The plant is an integrator followed by a lag, $\dfrac{1}{s\cdot s(s+1)}$: a pulse of area 0.5 gives a long-term velocity of 0.5, so $x$ grows like $0.5t$ ✔. (ii) At $t=1$: $x(1)=0.3935+0.5-0.875=0.0185$, which equals $h(0.5)=-e^{-0.5}+0.125-0.5+1=0.0185$ (the first step alone, since the second has not yet contributed) ✔.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$x(t)=e^{-(t-1)}-e^{-(t-0.5)}+0.5t-0.875=0.3935\,e^{1-t}+0.5t-0.875$ for $t>1$.

*Real-world:* a motor with a lag driving a position integrator, switched on for half a second: the position keeps creeping at the velocity it gained.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- Forgetting the time shift in each term (use $t-0.5$ and $t-1$, not $t$).
- Writing $x(t)$ for $t>1$ without subtracting the second step.

</Traps>

<Problem id="MT1 F2007 Q2" title="Two-mass system: transfer function from the force to the velocity of mass 1 (6 marks)" tags="elimination, velocity output, anti-resonance">

<Statement>

Given $m_1\ddot x_1+k_2(x_1-x_2)+k_1x_1=f$ and $m_2\ddot x_2+k_2(x_2-x_1)+b\dot x_2=0$, find the transfer function from $f$ to the velocity of mass 1 (zero initial conditions).

</Statement>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Laplace transform">

$(m_1s^2+k_1+k_2)X_1-k_2X_2=F$ and $(m_2s^2+bs+k_2)X_2-k_2X_1=0$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Solve the second equation for X₂">

$X_2=\dfrac{k_2X_1}{m_2s^2+bs+k_2}$.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Substitute into the first">

$(m_1s^2+k_1+k_2)X_1-\dfrac{k_2^2X_1}{m_2s^2+bs+k_2}=F\Rightarrow$
$$\frac{X_1}{F}=\frac{m_2s^2+bs+k_2}{(m_1s^2+k_1+k_2)(m_2s^2+bs+k_2)-k_2^2}.$$

</Step>
<Step title="4. Velocity">

$v_1=\dot x_1\Rightarrow V_1=sX_1$:
$$\frac{V_1}{F}=\frac{m_2s^3+bs^2+k_2s}{(m_1s^2+k_1+k_2)(m_2s^2+bs+k_2)-k_2^2}.$$

</Step>
<Step title="5. Checks">

(i) The denominator is 4th order (two masses, four states) ✔. (ii) At DC ($s\to0$) the velocity response $\to0$: a spring-held system has no steady velocity ✔. (iii) If $k_2=0$ the masses decouple and $V_1/F\to\dfrac{s}{m_1s^2+k_1}$ ✔.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$\dfrac{V_1}{F}=\dfrac{s\,(m_2s^2+bs+k_2)}{(m_1s^2+k_1+k_2)(m_2s^2+bs+k_2)-k_2^2}$.

*Real-world:* a machine on an isolator with an absorber ($m_2,b,k_2$): the zeros at the roots of $m_2s^2+bs+k_2$ are the "anti-resonance" the absorber creates.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- Forgetting the extra factor $s$ to get the velocity.
- Mixing the sign of the coupling term $k_2$.

</Traps>

<Source>official ✔ (identical).</Source>

<Problem id="MT1 F2007 Q3" title="Matlab residue output (1 mark)" tags="residue, repeated poles">

<Statement>

`[r,p,k]=residue(num,den)` returns $r=[1;0;1]$, $p=[-1;0;0]$, $k=[\ ]$. Which expansion is correct? (a) $\dfrac1{s+1}+\dfrac0s+\dfrac1{s^2}$; (b) $\dfrac1{s+1}+\dfrac1s+\dfrac0{s^2}$; (c) neither; (d) both; (e) none.

</Statement>

<Steps>
<Step title="Reasoning">

Matlab pairs each residue with its pole in order, and for a **repeated pole it lists the first-order term first, then the higher powers**: for the double pole at 0 the residues $r_2=0$, $r_3=1$ belong to $0/s$ and $1/s^2$. So the expansion is $\dfrac1{s+1}+\dfrac0s+\dfrac1{s^2}$.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

(a).

</Answer>

<Traps>

- Reading the residues as $\tfrac1{s+1}+\tfrac1s+\tfrac0{s^2}$: for a repeated pole the residues come in ascending power (the $1/s$ term first, then $1/s^2$).

</Traps>

<Source>official ✔ (answer a).</Source>
