<Problem id="MT1 W2009 Q1" title="Block-diagram reduction: a motor-driven mass–spring–damper with back-emf feedback (20 marks)" tags="block diagrams, series, feedback loop">

<Statement>

$V(s)$ enters a summing junction (+, with the feedback −). Forward path: $\dfrac{1}{Ls+R}\to K_f\to\dfrac{1}{ms^2+bs+k}\to X(s)$. Feedback path from $X(s)$: a block $K_es$ (velocity feedback) back to the junction. Use block-diagram reduction to find $X(s)/V(s)$.

</Statement>

<Think>

Three blocks in series just **multiply** into one forward block; then use the golden formula for one loop, **closed loop $=\dfrac{\text{forward}}{1+\text{forward}\times\text{feedback}}$**. Physically it is a motor-driven mass–spring–damper: voltage $\to$ current $\left(\tfrac1{Ls+R}\right)\to$ force $(K_f)\to$ position, with the back-emf $K_e\,sX$ fed back.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Multiply the series blocks">

$P_1=\dfrac{K_f}{(Ls+R)(ms^2+bs+k)}$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Feedback block">

$P_2=K_es$.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Loop formula">

$\dfrac XV=\dfrac{P_1}{1+P_1P_2}=\dfrac{1}{1/P_1+P_2}$ (dividing top and bottom by $P_1$ avoids nested fractions).

</Step>
<Step title="4. Expand the denominator of 1/P₁">

$(Ls+R)(ms^2+bs+k)=mLs^3+(bL+mR)s^2+(kL+bR)s+kR$.

</Step>
<Step title="5. Add the feedback term over K_f">

$\dfrac1{P_1}+P_2=\dfrac{mLs^3+(bL+mR)s^2+(kL+bR)s+kR+K_fK_es}{K_f}$.

</Step>
<Step title="6. Result">

$$\frac{X}{V}=\frac{K_f}{mLs^3+(bL+mR)s^2+(kL+bR+K_fK_e)s+kR}.$$

</Step>
<Step title="7. Checks">

(i) $K_e=0$: $\dfrac{K_f}{(Ls+R)(ms^2+bs+k)}$, the forward path alone ✔. (ii) DC gain $K_f/(kR)$: a voltage $V$ gives current $V/R$, force $K_fV/R$, static deflection $K_fV/(Rk)$ ✔. (iii) $K_fK_e$ appears **only in the $s^1$ coefficient**: the back-emf acts like extra damping.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$\dfrac{X}{V}=\dfrac{K_f}{mLs^3+(bL+mR)s^2+(kL+bR+K_fK_e)s+kR}$.

*Real-world:* a voice-coil or solenoid positioner: the faster it moves, the more the back-emf brakes it.

</Answer>

<Callout kind="warn" title="⚠ Official solution typo">

The final line of the handwritten solution prints $K_pK_e$ where the problem's symbols give $K_fK_e$ ($K_p$ is a slip for $K_f$). Everything else (the $bR+kL$ and $kR$ terms) matches.

</Callout>

<Traps>

- Forgetting the $K_f$ in the $s$-coefficient of the feedback term.
- Using $1-P_1P_2$ (positive feedback): the junction **subtracts** the feedback.

</Traps>

<Source>official solution ✔ up to one symbol typo.</Source>

</Problem>

<Problem id="MT1 W2009 Q2" title="Partial fractions: F(s) = (3s² + 2s + 2)/[(s+2)(s²+2s+5)] (25 marks)" tags="complex poles, completing the square">

<Statement>

Find $f(t)$ for $F(s)=\dfrac{3s^2+2s+2}{(s+2)(s^2+2s+5)}$.

</Statement>

<Think>

One real pole ($-2$) and one complex pair ($-1\pm2j$). Split into "a real-pole term" plus "a second-order term with a linear numerator", then rewrite the second-order term as a shifted cosine/sine pair using $(s+1)^2+2^2$.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Set up">

$F=\dfrac{A}{s+2}+\dfrac{Bs+C}{s^2+2s+5}$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Cover-up for the real pole">

$A=\dfrac{3\cdot4-4+2}{4-4+5}=\dfrac{10}{5}=2$.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Match numerators">

$3s^2+2s+2=2(s^2+2s+5)+(Bs+C)(s+2)=(2+B)s^2+(4+2B+C)s+(10+2C)$. $s^2$: $B=1$; $s^0$: $10+2C=2\Rightarrow C=-4$; check $s^1$: $4+2-4=2$ ✔.

</Step>
<Step title="4. Complete the square">

$s^2+2s+5=(s+1)^2+2^2$ and $s-4=(s+1)-5=(s+1)-\tfrac52\cdot2$:
$$F=\frac{2}{s+2}+\frac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+2^2}-\frac52\cdot\frac{2}{(s+1)^2+2^2}.$$

</Step>
<Step title="5. Table">

$\dfrac1{s+a}\leftrightarrow e^{-at}$, $\dfrac{s+a}{(s+a)^2+\omega^2}\leftrightarrow e^{-at}\cos\omega t$, $\dfrac{\omega}{(s+a)^2+\omega^2}\leftrightarrow e^{-at}\sin\omega t$:
$$f(t)=2e^{-2t}+e^{-t}\cos2t-\tfrac52e^{-t}\sin2t.$$

</Step>
<Step title="6. Checks">

Initial value: $f(0)=2+1=3$ and $\lim_{s\to\infty}sF=3$ ✔. Final value 0 (all poles stable) ✔.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$f(t)=2e^{-2t}+e^{-t}\cos2t-\tfrac52e^{-t}\sin2t$: a decaying exponential plus a damped oscillation at 2 rad/s.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- After completing the square the sine needs numerator $\omega=2$, so $-5=-\tfrac52\cdot2$.
- Always check the $s^1$ coefficient when matching.

</Traps>

<Source>official ✔ (identical).</Source>

<Problem id="MT1 W2009 Q3" title="Spring–mass–damper with a series spring and dashpot: Y/U, ODE, state space (30 marks)" tags="massless junction, Maxwell element, input derivative">

<Statement>

Mass $m=0.1$ kg hangs from the ceiling by a spring $k_1=6$ N/m and also from a branch consisting of a spring $k_2=4$ N/m in series with a dashpot $b=0.4$ N·s/m (dashpot top fixed to the ceiling; its lower end is the massless point $z$, joined to $k_2$). Force $u$ on the mass; $y$ and $z$ measured from equilibrium; zero initial conditions; output $y$. (a) $Y/U$; (b) the equivalent differential equation in $y$ and $u$; (c) one valid state-space representation.

</Statement>

<Think>

The series spring–dashpot is a **Maxwell element**: the same force passes through both. The junction $z$ is **massless**, so the two forces there balance. Eliminating $z$ gives a **third-order** system (three energy stores: two springs and the mass).

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="(a) 1. Mass">

$m\ddot y+k_1y+k_2(y-z)=u$ (E1).

</Step>
<Step title="(a) 2. Massless junction">

Spring pulls with $k_2(y-z)$, dashpot with $b\dot z$: $k_2(y-z)=b\dot z$ (E2).

</Step>
<Step title="(a) 3. Laplace">

(E1): $[ms^2+k_1+k_2]Y=k_2Z+U$. (E2): $k_2Y=(k_2+bs)Z\Rightarrow Z=\dfrac{k_2Y}{k_2+bs}$.

</Step>
<Step title="(a) 4. Substitute Z">

$\Big[ms^2+k_1+k_2-\dfrac{k_2^2}{k_2+bs}\Big]Y=U\Rightarrow\big[(ms^2+k_1+k_2)(k_2+bs)-k_2^2\big]Y=(k_2+bs)U$.

</Step>
<Step title="(a) 5. Expand">

$(ms^2+k_1+k_2)(k_2+bs)-k_2^2=mbs^3+mk_2s^2+(k_1+k_2)bs+k_1k_2$ (the $k_2^2$ cancels against $(k_1+k_2)k_2$ leaving $k_1k_2$).

</Step>
<Step title="(a) 6. Numbers">

$mb=0.04$, $mk_2=0.4$, $(k_1+k_2)b=4$, $k_1k_2=24$: $\dfrac{0.4s+4}{0.04s^3+0.4s^2+4s+24}$. Divide by $0.04$:
$$\frac YU=\frac{10s+100}{s^3+10s^2+100s+600}.$$

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Differential equation">

$\dddot y+10\ddot y+100\dot y+600y=10\dot u+100u$.

</Step>
<Step title="(c) Why the naive choice fails">

The right side has $\dot u$, so $x=(y,\dot y,\ddot y)$ would give a state equation containing $\dot u$: not a valid state model. Use Ogata's "$\beta$" method.

</Step>
<Step title="(c) The β's">

Standard form $a_1=10$, $a_2=100$, $a_3=600$; $b_0=b_1=0$, $b_2=10$, $b_3=100$. $\beta_0=b_0=0$; $\beta_1=b_1-a_1\beta_0=0$; $\beta_2=b_2-a_1\beta_1-a_2\beta_0=10$; $\beta_3=b_3-a_1\beta_2-a_2\beta_1-a_3\beta_0=100-100=0$.

</Step>
<Step title="(c) States and matrices">

$x_1=y-\beta_0u=y$; $x_2=\dot x_1-\beta_1u=\dot y$; $x_3=\dot x_2-\beta_2u=\ddot y-10u$. Then $\dot x_1=x_2$, $\dot x_2=x_3+10u$, $\dot x_3=-600x_1-100x_2-10x_3+\beta_3u$ ($\beta_3=0$):
$$\dot x=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\\-600&-100&-10\end{bmatrix}x+\begin{bmatrix}0\\10\\0\end{bmatrix}u,\qquad y=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\end{bmatrix}x.$$
Check: converting back gives exactly $\dfrac{10s+100}{s^3+10s^2+100s+600}$ ✔. Here $x_3$ is "acceleration minus $10u$", an auxiliary variable that removes the input derivative.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

(a) $\dfrac{10s+100}{s^3+10s^2+100s+600}$; (b) $\dddot y+10\ddot y+100\dot y+600y=10\dot u+100u$; (c) the matrices above.

*Real-world:* a vehicle seat or mount with a Maxwell element: the series damper "lets go" at low frequency, so at DC the static stiffness is just $k_1$ (DC gain $100/600=1/6=1/k_1$ ✔).

</Answer>

<Traps>

- Treating $z$ as having mass (it has none: the forces balance).
- Choosing $x_3=\ddot y$ and getting a $\dot u$ term in the state equation (ill-posed).
- The spring deflection is $y-z$ (sign).

</Traps>

<Source>official ✔ (verified numerically).</Source>

<Problem id="MT1 W2009 Q4" title="Multiple choice and short questions (25 marks)" tags="IVT, pulley, momentum, terminology, Matlab">

<Statement>

Five short items: initial value theorem; equations of motion of a spring–mass–pulley system; a bullet fired into a block on a spring; names of the elements of a feedback diagram; matching Matlab commands.

</Statement>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Initial-value theorem">

(b) $f(0^+)=\lim_{s\to\infty}sF(s)$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Pulley with inertia">

The equations require **(e) a, b and c**: the sum of vertical forces on the mass, the sum of vertical forces on the pulley, and the sum of moments about the pulley centre. **Not (d)**: the tension is *not* constant along the string for a pulley with inertia (it changes across the pulley).

</Step>
<Step title="3. Bullet in a block">

$m=0.02$ kg, $v=600$ m/s into $M=5$ kg on a spring ($k=200$ N/m). The bullet embeds, so momentum is conserved just after impact: $(m+M)v'=mv\Rightarrow v'=\dfrac{0.02\cdot600}{5.02}=2.390$ m/s: **(a)**.

</Step>
<Step title="4. Feedback diagram names">

$G$ = direct (forward) transfer function; $C$ = controlled output; $H$ = feedback transfer function; $R$ = reference input; $B$ = primary feedback signal; $E$ = error (actuating) signal.

</Step>
<Step title="5. Matlab matches">

(a) response to $\delta(t)$: `impulse`; (b) response to $1(t)$: `step`; (c) TF to state space: `tf2ss`; (d) partial fractions: `residue`; (e) arbitrary input: `lsim`.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

(1) b; (2) e; (3) a, 2.390 m/s; (4) as listed; (5) impulse, step, tf2ss, residue, lsim.
