<Problem id="HW5 Q1" title="Magnetic levitation train: EOM, equilibrium, linearisation, transfer function" tags="Taylor series, unstable vs stable convention">

<Statement>

The levitation force is $F_L=k\,i^2/z^2$ ($z$ = air gap, $i$ = coil current), opposing the weight $mg$. (a) Derive the EOM. (b) Equilibrium $z_0,i_0$. (c) Linearise with $\Delta z=z-z_0$, $\Delta i=i-i_0$. (d) Transfer function.

</Statement>

<Think>

Nonlinear, two variables. Newton for the car, find the equilibrium by setting $\ddot z=0$, then a **two-variable Taylor expansion** with the equilibrium relation used to eliminate $k,m$. The sign convention of $z$ decides stable vs unstable.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. EOM (gap measured upward, the T06 convention)">

$m\ddot z=\dfrac{ki^2}{z^2}-mg$, i.e. $\ddot z=f(z,i)=\dfrac km\dfrac{i^2}{z^2}-g$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Equilibrium">

$\ddot z=0$: $\dfrac{ki_0^2}{z_0^2}=mg\Rightarrow i_0=z_0\sqrt{mg/k}$.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Partial derivatives">

Using $\dfrac{ki_0^2}{mz_0^2}=g$: $\;\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial z}=-\dfrac{2ki_0^2}{mz_0^3}=-\dfrac{2g}{z_0}$, $\;\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial i}=\dfrac{2ki_0}{mz_0^2}=\dfrac{2g}{i_0}$.

</Step>
<Step title="4. Linear model and transfer function">

$$\Delta\ddot z=-\frac{2g}{z_0}\Delta z+\frac{2g}{i_0}\Delta i,\qquad \frac{\Delta Z}{\Delta I}=\frac{2g/i_0}{s^2+2g/z_0}.$$

Poles $\pm j\sqrt{2g/z_0}$ (44.3 rad/s for $z_0=10$ mm): an undamped oscillator.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$m\ddot z=ki^2/z^2-mg$; $i_0=z_0\sqrt{mg/k}$; $\Delta\ddot z=-\tfrac{2g}{z_0}\Delta z+\tfrac{2g}{i_0}\Delta i$; $\dfrac{\Delta Z}{\Delta I}=\dfrac{2g/i_0}{s^2+2g/z_0}$.

</Answer>

<Callout kind="warn" title="⚠ Anthony measured z downward">

His EOM $m\ddot z=mg-ki^2/z^2$ gives $\Delta Z/\Delta I=-\dfrac{2g/i_0}{s^2-2g/z_0}$, a real **right-half-plane pole** (unstable). His algebra after the first line is consistent, but the course convention (T06: *gap measured upward*; the force grows when the gap shrinks) gives the oscillator. State the convention first.

</Callout>

<Traps>

- Use the equilibrium relation to eliminate $k$ and $m$ from the derivatives ($2g/z_0$ and $2g/i_0$).
- The linear model has no damping: a real controller must add derivative action.

</Traps>

</Problem>

<Problem id="HW5 Q2" title="Transfer functions of four circuits" tags="impedance method, op-amps, twin-T notch">

<Statement>

Find the transfer function of (a) a passive divider: $R_1\parallel C$ in series with the signal, $R_2$ to ground (output across $R_2$); (b) an inverting op-amp with $R_2$ in series with $(R_1\parallel C)$ at the input and $R_f$ in the feedback; (c) an inverting op-amp with $R_{in}$ at the input and $R_2$ in series with $(R_1\parallel C)$ in the feedback; (d) a twin-T network.

</Statement>

<Think>

Use impedances $Z_R=R$, $Z_C=1/(Cs)$ and the divider / op-amp rule $-Z_f/Z_{in}$. For (d) there are two internal nodes: **KCL at every node**.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="(a) Divider">

$Z_1=\dfrac{R_1}{1+R_1Cs}$: $\;\dfrac YU=\dfrac{R_2}{R_2+Z_1}=\dfrac{R_2(1+R_1Cs)}{R_1+R_2+R_1R_2Cs}=\dfrac{Cs+1/R_1}{Cs+1/R_1+1/R_2}$. DC gain $R_2/(R_1+R_2)$; high-frequency gain 1 (the capacitor bypasses $R_1$).

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Inverting, input impedance a series pair">

$Z_{in}=R_2+\dfrac{R_1}{1+R_1Cs}$: $\;\dfrac{V_o}{V_{in}}=-\dfrac{R_f}{Z_{in}}=-\dfrac{R_f(1+R_1Cs)}{R_1+R_2+R_1R_2Cs}$.

</Step>
<Step title="(c) Inverting, feedback impedance a series pair">

$Z_f=R_2+\dfrac{R_1}{1+R_1Cs}$: $\;\dfrac{V_o}{V_{in}}=-\dfrac{Z_f}{R_{in}}=-\dfrac{R_1+R_2+R_1R_2Cs}{R_{in}(1+R_1Cs)}$.

</Step>
<Step title="(d) Twin-T: nodal equations">

With $a=RCs$ and $S=V_{in}+V_{out}$: low-pass T middle node $V_{a1}=\dfrac{S}{2(1+a)}$, high-pass T middle node $V_{a2}=\dfrac{aS}{2(1+a)}$; at the output $(V_{a1}-V_{out})+a(V_{a2}-V_{out})=0$. Result

$$\frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}}=\frac{1+a^2}{1+4a+a^2}=\frac{R^2C^2s^2+1}{R^2C^2s^2+4RCs+1}:$$

a **notch**: zeros at $\pm j/RC$, real poles at $(-2\pm\sqrt3)/RC$.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

(a)–(d) as above.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- Anthony stopped before simplifying (d); the unsimplified form $\dfrac{C^2s^2+1/R^2}{\ldots}$ is equivalent.
- Watch which element is at the *input* and which is in the *feedback* of the op-amp.

</Traps>

<Problem id="HW5 Q3" title="Mobility and impedance analogs of a two-mass network" tags="mobility, impedance, duality">

<Statement>

Mass $M_{M1}$ hangs from the ceiling by spring $C_{M1}$ and dashpot $R_{M1}$ in parallel; mass $M_{M2}$ hangs from $M_{M1}$ by $C_{M2}\parallel R_{M2}$ and *also* from the ceiling by a long spring $C_{M3}$ and long dashpot $R_{M3}$. A force $Fe^{i\omega t}$ acts on $M_{M2}$. Draw the mobility and impedance analogs.

</Statement>

<Think>

Chapter 10 recipe: nodes = velocities of the two masses plus ground; start with the force source on node 2; wall elements go to ground; elements between the masses go between the nodes.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Mobility circuit">

Node 2 ($M_{M2}$): current source $F$, capacitor $M_{M2}$, inductor $C_{M3}$ and resistor $1/R_{M3}$ to ground; between node 2 and node 1: inductor $C_{M2}$ in parallel with resistor $1/R_{M2}$; node 1 ($M_{M1}$): capacitor $M_{M1}$, inductor $C_{M1}$, resistor $1/R_{M1}$ to ground.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Check by KCL">

Node 1: $M_1\ddot x_1+R_{M1}\dot x_1+k_1x_1+R_{M2}(\dot x_1-\dot x_2)+k_2(x_1-x_2)=0$; node 2: $M_2\ddot x_2+R_{M2}(\dot x_2-\dot x_1)+k_2(x_2-x_1)+R_{M3}\dot x_2+k_3x_2=F$ ✔.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Impedance circuit (the dual)">

Two meshes (current = velocity, force = voltage). Mesh 2: voltage source $F$ in series with inductor $M_{M2}$, capacitor $C_{M3}$, resistor $R_{M3}$. Mesh 1: inductor $M_{M1}$, capacitor $C_{M1}$, resistor $R_{M1}$. The meshes **share** a branch: capacitor $C_{M2}$ in series with resistor $R_{M2}$ (carrying the relative motion $\dot x_1-\dot x_2$).

</Step>
</Steps>

<Problem id="HW5 Q4" title="Vertical device with a velocity source; effect of a rigid rod" tags="velocity source, rigid link">

<Statement>

A velocity source $U_0e^{i\omega t}$ drives spring $C_{M1}$, then bar $M_{M1}$; a cylinder (mass $M_{M2}$) contains a piston on a stem with friction $R_M$ and a spring $C_{M2}$. Neglect the fluid in the cavity. (a) Mobility analog. (b) Convert to the impedance analog. (c) A short, massless, infinitely rigid rod is attached between $M_1$ and $M_2$: how do the circuits change?

</Statement>

<Steps>
<Step title="(a) Mobility">

Voltage source $U_0e^{i\omega t}$ (a velocity source) in series with inductor $C_{M1}$ to node 1; capacitor $M_{M1}$ from node 1 to ground; between node 1 and node 2 the parallel pair inductor $C_{M2}$ and resistor $1/R_M$ (relative motion of piston and cylinder); capacitor $M_{M2}$ from node 2 to ground.

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Impedance (dual)">

A velocity source becomes a **current** source: $U_0e^{i\omega t}$ in parallel with capacitor $C_{M1}$ (to ground); inductor $M_{M1}$ in series; then to ground two parallel branches: ($C_{M2}$ in series with $R_M$) and inductor $M_{M2}$.

</Step>
<Step title="(c) The rigid rod">

It forces $v_1=v_2$. **Mobility:** the pair $C_{M2}\parallel1/R_M$ is short-circuited (no relative velocity); $M_{M1}$ and $M_{M2}$ end up in parallel at one node: a single mass $M_{M1}+M_{M2}$ hanging from $C_{M1}$. **Impedance:** the branch $C_{M2}$–$R_M$ carries no current and is removed; $M_{M1}$ and $M_{M2}$ are in series (equal velocity = equal current): total inductance $M_{M1}+M_{M2}$.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

As above. *Real-world:* a shaker pushing a mass through a spring with a tuned-damper absorber: once locked by a rigid rod, the absorber stops absorbing and the system is just one bigger mass.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- A **velocity source is a voltage source in the mobility analog but a current source in the impedance analog.** Anthony drew a voltage source again in (b).
- The rod removes the spring/damper pair, not the masses.

</Traps>

<Problem id="HW5 Q5" title="Lever with spring, damper and force: equivalent circuit with transformers" tags="lever, parallel axis, transformer 3:1">

<Statement>

A uniform bar (mass $m$, length $L$) pivots at $O$, a distance $L/4$ from its left end. A spring $k$ pulls the left end up (displacement $x$); at the right end a force $F(t)$ acts down and a dashpot $c$ to the ground. Leaving transformers in, draw a valid equivalent circuit; identify each element, the potential and the flux.

</Statement>

<Think>

This is $\text{MT2 F2022 Q1a}$ (Chapter 10, Example 3). Rotation about a pin ⇒ moments; the lever arms $L/4$ and $3L/4$ give a **3 : 1** transformer.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Kinematics">

Left end up $x=\tfrac L4\theta$, right end down $y=\tfrac{3L}4\theta$, so $\dot y=3\dot x$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Inertia about the pivot">

$I_O=I_G+m\big(\tfrac L4\big)^2=\tfrac{mL^2}{12}+\tfrac{mL^2}{16}=\dfrac{7mL^2}{48}$.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Moments about O">

$$\frac{7mL^2}{48}\ddot\theta+\frac{9cL^2}{16}\dot\theta+\frac{kL^2}{16}\theta=\frac{3L}4F .$$

Referred to the right end (velocity $\dot y$): equivalent mass $7m/27$. Referred to the left end: $\tfrac{7m}3\ddot x+9c\dot x+kx=3F$.

</Step>
<Step title="4. Circuit with a transformer (mobility analog: potential = velocity, flux = force)">

Node R (right end, potential $\dot y$): current source $F$, resistor $1/c$, capacitor $7m/27$ (the bar's inertia referred to the right end); an ideal transformer with voltage ratio $3:1$ ($\dot y:\dot x$) and force ratio $1:3$; node L (left end, potential $\dot x$): inductor $1/k$ to ground.

</Step>
<Step title="5. Rotational node instead">

Potential $\dot\theta$, flux torque: current source $3FL/4$ in parallel with resistor $16/(9cL^2)$, capacitor $7mL^2/48$, inductor $16/(kL^2)$.

</Step>
</Steps>

</Answer>

<Callout kind="warn" title="⚠ Errors around this problem">

- The official solution to the identical $\text{MT2 F2022 Q1a}$ labels the impedance capacitor "$kL^2/16$" (the rotational *stiffness*); the capacitor is the compliance $16/(kL^2)$. Its "alternative" EOM mixes coordinates; the correct left-end equation is $(m+16I_G/L^2)\ddot x+9c\dot x+kx=3F$.
- **Anthony** lumped the bar into two point masses ($\tfrac34m$ and $\tfrac14m$) without deriving them from $I_O$: the true referred inertia is $7m/27=0.26m$, while his $M_1+M_2/9=0.78m$. He also drew the damper as "$1/c$" in the impedance analog (it is $c$ there). His transformer ratio 3 : 1 and the structure are right.

</Callout>

<Traps>

- The bar's inertia is $I_G+md^2$ about $O$: not $mL^2/12$, not $mL^2/3$.
- Potentials and fluxes scale oppositely through the transformer (velocity ×3, force ÷3). Referred to the right end, the spring's compliance becomes $9/k$.

</Traps>
