<Problem id="HW3 Q1" title="Block-diagram simplification: three integrators; nested positive loops" tags="block algebra, Mason">

<Statement>

Simplify (a) and (b) to find $G(s)=Y(s)/U(s)$. (a) $U$ enters a summer, then three integrators $1/s$ in series with outputs $X_1,X_2,X_3$; $X_1,X_2,X_3$ feed back through $-a_1,-a_2,-a_3$ to the summer; the output summer adds $b_1X_1+b_2X_2+b_3X_3$. (b) Chain $U\to\Sigma_1\to H_1\to\Sigma_2\to H_2\to\Sigma_3\to H_3\to\Sigma_4\to Y$, a feed-forward $H_4$ from node 2 to $\Sigma_4$, and feedbacks $H_5$ (node 4 to $\Sigma_1$), $H_6$ (node 5 to $\Sigma_2$), $H_7$ ($Y$ to $\Sigma_3$); all summers positive.

</Statement>

<Think>

(a) is the controllable-canonical realisation of a third-order TF (integrator equations, then eliminate). (b) is tangled enough that **Mason's rule** is faster than moving pick-off points: list paths, loops, find which loops touch.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="(a) Integrator equations">

$sX_1=U-a_1X_1-a_2X_2-a_3X_3$, $X_2=X_1/s$, $X_3=X_1/s^2$. So $U=\dfrac{s^3+a_1s^2+a_2s+a_3}{s^2}X_1$ and $Y=\dfrac{b_1s^2+b_2s+b_3}{s^2}X_1$.

$$G(s)=\frac{b_1s^2+b_2s+b_3}{s^3+a_1s^2+a_2s+a_3}.$$

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Paths">

$P_1=H_1H_2H_3$ (nodes 1–2–4–5–6) and $P_2=H_4$ (nodes 1–2–3–6).

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Loops">

$L_1=H_1H_5$, $L_2=H_2H_6$, $L_3=H_3H_7$, $L_4=H_4H_7H_6H_5$ (through nodes 2–3–6–5–4–2).

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Non-touching loops">

Only $L_1$ and $L_3$ share no node. So $\Delta=1-(L_1+L_2+L_3+L_4)+L_1L_3$.

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Cofactors">

Every loop touches $P_1$: $\Delta_1=1$. Only $L_2$ misses $P_2$: $\Delta_2=1-H_2H_6$.

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Result">

$$G=\frac{H_1H_2H_3+H_4(1-H_2H_6)}{1-H_1H_5-H_2H_6-H_3H_7-H_4H_5H_6H_7+H_1H_3H_5H_7}.$$

*Check:* random values for $H_1\dots H_7$ give the same number from Mason and from solving the node equations ($-1.3069$).

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

(a) $\dfrac{b_1s^2+b_2s+b_3}{s^3+a_1s^2+a_2s+a_3}$; (b) the Mason expression above.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- In (b) the typed formula in the scan has "$H_4H_7H_6H_8$"; it means $H_5$.
- With all summers positive the loop gains carry no extra minus signs; non-touching means *no common node*.

</Traps>

<Problem id="HW3 Q2" title="Bridged-T network: poles, zeros, the dip, and how to reduce it" tags="step response, zeros, cookbook formulas">

<Statement>

$\dfrac{V_o}{V_{in}}=\dfrac{1+2R_1Cs+R_1R_2C^2s^2}{1+(2R_1+R_2)Cs+R_1R_2C^2s^2}$. (a) Pole-zero diagram for $R_1=0.5$, $R_2=1$, $C=0.5$. (b) Unit-step response. (c) Peak time and overshoot: are the "cookbook" equations accurate? (d) How to reduce the overshoot without changing the peak time much?

</Statement>

<Think>

Substitute numbers first. The poles will tell whether the cookbook ($\zeta$) formulas apply at all. The step response starts at 1 (numerator and denominator have the same degree), which hints at a *dip*, not an overshoot.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="(a) Numbers, poles, zeros">

$2R_1C=0.5$, $R_1R_2C^2=0.125$, $(2R_1+R_2)C=1$: $G=\dfrac{0.125s^2+0.5s+1}{0.125s^2+s+1}=\dfrac{s^2+4s+8}{s^2+8s+8}$. **Zeros** $-2\pm j2$; **poles** $-4\pm2\sqrt2=-1.172$ and $-6.828$ (both real).

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Step response">

$G=1-\dfrac{4s}{s^2+8s+8}$, so $Y=\dfrac1s-\dfrac4{(s+1.172)(s+6.828)}$ and, with $p_2-p_1=4\sqrt2$:

$$y(t)=1-\tfrac{\sqrt2}2\big(e^{-1.172t}-e^{-6.828t}\big).$$

$y(0^+)=1$ (direct feedthrough), then it **dips** and recovers to 1: an *undershoot*.

</Step>
<Step title="(c) Extremum and cookbook">

$t_p=\dfrac{\ln(p_2/p_1)}{p_2-p_1}=\dfrac{\ln5.828}{5.657}=0.312$ s; $y(t_p)=0.594$: a **41 % dip**, no overshoot (the response never exceeds 1). The denominator alone has $\zeta=\sqrt2>1$ (overdamped), so the cookbook formulas $e^{-\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}$ and $\pi/\omega_d$ don't even apply. They fail because the dip is created by the **zeros**.

</Step>
<Step title="(d) The right lever">

$G=1-\dfrac{R_2Cs}{D(s)}$: the whole dip is the term $R_2Cs/D$. Make $R_2$ small and $R_1$ large (the zero damping is $\sqrt{R_1/R_2}$). Simulation (minimum, time): $(R_1,R_2)=(0.5,1)$: 0.593 at 0.315 s; $(1,0.5)$: 0.829 at 0.285 s; $(2,0.5)$: **0.901 at 0.357 s** (dip cut from 41 % to 10 % with the extremum still near 0.3 s); $(0.5,0.1)$: 0.918 at 0.077 s. Changing $C$ only rescales time.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

Zeros $-2\pm2j$, poles $-1.172,-6.828$; $y=1-\tfrac{\sqrt2}2(e^{-1.172t}-e^{-6.828t})$; minimum 0.594 at 0.312 s (41 % undershoot, no overshoot), cookbook not applicable; reduce by lowering $R_2$ and raising $R_1$ (e.g. $R_1=2$, $R_2=0.5$ gives a 10 % dip).

</Answer>

<Traps>

- "Overshoot" = largest deviation from the final value: here it is *below*.
- Real poles with complex zeros: never apply the $\zeta$ formulas of the denominator alone.
- Anthony's answer to (d) ("increase the damping ratio") is the wrong lever: the poles are already overdamped; the dip lives in the zeros.

</Traps>

<Problem id="HW3 Q3" title="Tracking error and disturbance error versus gain K" tags="FVT, disturbance rejection, type 0">

<Statement>

$R\to(+,-)\to E\to K\to(+)\to\dfrac1{s^2+12s+15}\to Y$, with disturbance $T_d$ entering at the second summer; unity feedback. (a) $G=Y/R$. (b) Steady-state tracking error for a unit step, for $0\le K\le10$. (c) $H=Y/T_d$ with $R=0$. (d) Steady-state error for a unit step disturbance.

</Statement>

<Think>

Linear system: handle the two inputs separately (superposition). The error is $E=R-Y$. The FVT applies because the closed loop $s^2+12s+15+K$ is a stable second-order polynomial for every $K\ge0$.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="(a) Closed loop">

$G=\dfrac{KP}{1+KP}$ with $P=\dfrac1{s^2+12s+15}$: $\;G=\dfrac{K}{s^2+12s+15+K}$.

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Step tracking error">

$e_{ss}=1-G(0)=1-\dfrac K{15+K}=\dfrac{15}{15+K}$: 1 at $K=0$, 0.75 at $K=5$, 0.6 at $K=10$.

</Step>
<Step title="(c) Disturbance transfer function">

With $R=0$: $H=\dfrac{Y}{T_d}=\dfrac{1}{s^2+12s+15+K}$.

</Step>
<Step title="(d) Step-disturbance error">

$R=0\Rightarrow E=-Y$; $e_{ss}=-\lim s\cdot\dfrac1s\cdot\dfrac1{s^2+12s+15+K}=-\dfrac1{15+K}$: $-0.0667$, $-0.05$, $-0.04$.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$G=\dfrac K{s^2+12s+15+K}$; $e_{ss}=\dfrac{15}{15+K}$; $H=\dfrac1{s^2+12s+15+K}$; $e_{ss}=-\dfrac1{15+K}$.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- The disturbance enters *after* the controller, so raising $K$ shrinks it as $1/(15+K)$ but never to zero (no integrator).
- With $R=0$ the tracking error is $-Y$: negative.
- *Real-world:* cruise control with a plain P controller on a type-0 plant leaves a permanent error: that is why integral action exists (<Ch slug="pid">Chapter 17</Ch>).

</Traps>

<Problem id="HW3 Q4" title="Third-order multi-loop system and Simulink" tags="block reduction, canonical realisation">

<Statement>

$R$ feeds gains $b_3,b_2,b_1$ injected before each of three integrators; feedbacks $a_1$ (from $x_1$), $a_2$ (from $x_2$), $a_3$ (from $Y$) return negatively. (a) $T=Y/R$. (b) Simulink with $a=(6,11,6)$, $b=(1,2,3)$: step response for $0\le t\le12$ s.

</Statement>

<Think>

Write each integrator equation and eliminate; each $b_i$ enters at a different point in the chain so the numerator picks up $a_i$ terms.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Integrator equations">

$sx_1=b_3R-a_1x_1-a_2x_2-a_3Y$, $\;sx_2=x_1+b_2R$, $\;sY=x_2+b_1R$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Eliminate">

$Y\big[s^3+a_1s^2+a_2s+a_3\big]=R\big[b_1s^2+(a_1b_1+b_2)s+(a_2b_1+a_1b_2+b_3)\big]$.

$$T(s)=\frac{b_1s^2+(a_1b_1+b_2)s+(a_2b_1+a_1b_2+b_3)}{s^3+a_1s^2+a_2s+a_3}.$$

</Step>
<Step title="3. Numbers">

$T=\dfrac{s^2+8s+26}{s^3+6s^2+11s+6}=\dfrac{s^2+8s+26}{(s+1)(s+2)(s+3)}$. DC gain $26/6=4.33$. Monotone step response, 2 % settling time about 4 s (slowest pole $-1$).

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$T$ as above; the step response rises monotonically to 4.33 in about 5–6 s.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- The constant term of the numerator is $b_1a_2+b_2a_1+b_3$, not just $b_3$. The poles depend only on the $a$'s.

</Traps>

<Problem id="HW3 Q5" title="Products of first/second-order factors and their Bode plots" tags="Bode superposition, corner frequencies">

<Statement>

Write each TF as a product of first- and/or second-order systems, plot the Bode diagram of each factor and of the total, identify the corner frequencies. (a) $\dfrac1{(1+0.5s)(1+5s)}$; (b) $\dfrac{3(s^2+2.1s+1)}{(s-2)^2}$; (c) $\dfrac{s-2}{s^2+5s+4}$; (d) $\dfrac{12(s+5)}{s(s+3)(s+8)}$.

</Statement>

<Think>

Magnitudes add in dB and phases add in degrees (<Ch slug="frequency-basics">Chapter 6</Ch>). First **normalise each factor to $(1+s/\omega_c)$**, collect the constants, then read the corners. Right-half-plane factors have the *same* magnitude as their left-half-plane twins; only the phase sign flips.

</Think>

<Steps>
<Step title="(a) Two real poles">

Corners **2** rad/s $(1+0.5s)$ and **0.2** rad/s $(1+5s)$. DC gain 1 (0 dB). Slope 0, then $-20$ after 0.2, $-40$ after 2; phase $0^\circ\to-180^\circ$. (−3 dB bandwidth 0.198 rad/s.)

</Step>
<Step title="(b) Two real zeros, double RHP pole">

$G=0.75\,(s^2+2.1s+1)\dfrac1{(1-s/2)^2}$. The quadratic has $\zeta=1.05>1$: **two real zeros** at $-0.73$ and $-1.37$ (each $+20$ dB/dec). The denominator is a **double RHP pole at $+2$** (corner 2, $-40$ dB/dec, magnitude of $(1+s/2)^{-2}$). Magnitude: $-2.5$ dB (0.75) up to 0.73, rising, then flat after 2 rad/s, ending at $3=+9.5$ dB. Phase: the LHP zeros add $0\to+180^\circ$, each RHP pole adds $0\to+90^\circ$.

</Step>
<Step title="(c) RHP zero">

$G=-0.5\dfrac{1-s/2}{(1+s)(1+s/4)}$. Corners 1, 4 (poles), **2 (RHP zero)**. DC gain $-0.5$ ($-6$ dB); flat to 1, $-20$ on (1,2), flat on (2,4), $-20$ after 4. Phase starts at $\pm180^\circ$ (the minus sign); the RHP zero adds *lag* (unlike an LHP zero).

</Step>
<Step title="(d) Integrator, zero, two poles">

$G=2.5\dfrac{1+s/5}{s(1+s/3)(1+s/8)}$: corners 3, 5 (zero), 8, plus an integrator. Magnitude $2.5/\omega$ ($+7.96$ dB at 1 rad/s), $-40$ from 3, $-20$ from 5, $-40$ from 8. Phase: $-90^\circ\to-180^\circ$.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

Corner frequencies: (a) 0.2, 2; (b) 0.73, 1.37 (zeros), 2 (double RHP pole); (c) 1, 2, 4; (d) 3, 5, 8 with an integrator.

</Answer>

<Callout kind="warn" title="⚠ The official 'factors' for (b) are not standard Bode building blocks">

The official splits (b) as $\dfrac3{s-2}\cdot\dfrac{s^2+2.1s+1}{s-2}$; the second factor is *improper* (rising $+20$ dB/dec forever). Use proper first/second-order pieces in $(1+s/\omega)$ form. **Anthony** quoted Matlab `bandwidth` values where the question asked for *corner* frequencies (a "bandwidth" is where the exact curve falls 3 dB, not where the asymptotes meet).

</Callout>

<Traps>

- RHP poles/zeros: same magnitude, opposite phase sign (non-minimum phase).
- $s^2+2.1s+1$ has $\zeta=1.05$: real zeros, not an underdamped pair.

</Traps>
