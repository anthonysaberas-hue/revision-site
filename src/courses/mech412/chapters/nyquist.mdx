# Nyquist stability criterion

<div className="lede">

Routh needs the characteristic polynomial; Bode needs a minimum-phase loop. The **Nyquist criterion** needs neither: it decides stability from the frequency response alone by counting how many times a curve circles one special point, $-1$. It works for delays, for unstable open loops and for plants you only know from measurements.

</div>

<Idea title="The 30-second version">

The closed-loop poles are the zeros of $F(s)=1+L(s)$. Map the whole right half-plane boundary (the *Nyquist contour*) through $L(s)$ and count how many times the image encircles $-1$:

$$
\boxed{Z=N+P}
$$

$N$ = clockwise encirclements of $-1$, $P$ = open-loop poles in the right half-plane, $Z$ = **closed-loop poles in the right half-plane**. Stable closed loop ⇔ $Z=0$. If the open loop is stable ($P=0$) you simply need **no** encirclement of $-1$.

</Idea>

## Why it works (the argument principle, in words)

Walk once clockwise around a closed contour in the $s$-plane and watch $F(s)$: every zero of $F$ inside the contour makes the image wind once clockwise around the origin, every pole once counter-clockwise. So the image circles the origin $N=Z-P$ times (clockwise positive). Take the contour to be the whole right half-plane (up the imaginary axis, back along a huge semicircle). Then $Z$ = zeros of $F=1+L$ = closed-loop poles in the RHP, and $P$ = poles of $F$ = poles of $L$ in the RHP. Since $F=1+L$ the image of $F$ circling the origin is the image of $L$ circling $-1$.

**Convention check:** some textbooks write $N=P-Z$ for counter-clockwise counts. Use one convention throughout: here $N$ is *clockwise* and $Z=N+P$.

## Drawing the plot

1. Evaluate $L(j\omega)$ for $\omega$ from $0^+$ to $\infty$ (the polar plot of Chapter 19). The mirror image ($-\omega$) is the **complex conjugate**.
2. If $L$ has poles **on the imaginary axis** (an integrator at the origin), the contour must **indent** around them with a small semicircle, and the plot gets a **large arc**: $180^\circ$ clockwise for one integrator, $360^\circ$ for two ("assume the infinity arc is connected").
3. Count clockwise turns around $-1$.

## Examples

### 1. $L=\dfrac{K}{s(s^2+s+6)}$ (HW8-2024 Q3a)

$P=0$ (no RHP poles; the origin pole is handled by the indentation). For $K=1$ the curve does not circle $-1$: $N=0\Rightarrow Z=0$ **stable**. **Maximum $K$:** the curve crosses the real axis where $\operatorname{Im}L=0$, at $\omega^2=6$, with real part $u=-\dfrac K6$. Stability needs $-K/6>-1$: **$K<6$**. Check with Routh on $s^3+s^2+6s+K$: $K<6$, and at $K=6$ the roots are $\pm j\sqrt6$ and $-1$ ✔.

### 2. $L=\dfrac{K(s+1)}{s^2(s^2+6)}$ (HW8-2024 Q3b): unstable for every $K$

Two integrators (a $360^\circ$ arc) and a pair of poles at $\pm j\sqrt6$ (small indentations). The plot encircles $-1$ **twice clockwise**: $N=2$, $P=0$ ⇒ $Z=2$ (**unstable for all $K$**). Confirm with the characteristic polynomial $s^4+6s^2+Ks+K$: there is **no $s^3$ term**, so the Routh test fails for every $K$ (for $K=1$ the roots are $0.088\pm j2.42$ and $-0.088\pm j0.40$).

### 3. An unstable open loop: $L=\dfrac{K_1}{s(s-1)}$

Here $P=1$ (the pole at $+1$). We need $Z=0=N+P\Rightarrow N=-1$: **one counter-clockwise encirclement** of $-1$. Adding derivative feedback, $\dfrac{K_1(1+K_2s)}{s(s-1)}$, supplies the extra phase that produces that encirclement for large enough $K_1$.

### 4. Positive-phase systems and delays

$L=\dfrac{K(s-2)}{(s+1)^2}$ (a RHP zero) starts at $-2K$ on the real axis: the curve begins on the *left* of the origin, so for $K\ge\tfrac12$ the point $-1$ is encircled and the closed loop is unstable although the open loop is stable.

## Relative stability

The distance from the curve to $-1$ is the margin: the gain margin $1/|L(j\omega_{pc})|$ and phase margin $180^\circ+\angle L(j\omega_{gc})$ (Chapter 19) are just two ways to read that distance. A curve that hugs $-1$ gives a lightly damped closed loop even if technically stable.

## Compensator comparison on one plant (the lecture's recap)

$G=\dfrac{K}{s(s+1)}$ with four methods: lead by root locus (poles at $-1\pm j\sqrt3$ need $+30^\circ$, $a=10$, compensator pole near $-37$), lag by root locus ($K_v=10$), lead by Bode and lag by Bode (Chapter 20). The *time-domain* methods (root locus) specify poles; the *frequency-domain* methods specify margins; they give compatible designs.
