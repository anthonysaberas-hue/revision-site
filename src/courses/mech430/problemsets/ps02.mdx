# 1-D isentropic flow

<p className="lede">Eleven problems on the toolbox of Chapter 5. Almost all of them are the same three moves: find M at the known station, compute the invariants T₀, p₀, A* there, and carry them to the new station. γ = 1.4 unless stated; table readings are Table A2 (nearest row).</p>

<Remember title="The invariants recipe (Jan 27 lecture)">

1. Find $M$ where you know the state ($V/\sqrt{\gamma RT}$, or $p/p_0$, or from $\dot m$).
2. Compute $T_0$, $p_0$, $A^*$ there. They are constant along an isentropic duct.
3. At the new station use the new $M$ / $p$ / $A$ with the same invariants. See [Chapter 5](#/mech430/ch/isentropic).

</Remember>

<Problem id="PS2 P1" title="Terminology: Mach, static, stagnation, sonic, choking" tags="definitions">

<Statement>

Define in one sentence: (a) Mach number, (b) static conditions, (c) stagnation (total) conditions, (d) sonic (critical) conditions, (e) choking.

</Statement>

<Think>

Each is a *reference state* or a limit. The two words that matter: stagnation is reached **isentropically**, and choking is about **mass flow** not rising when the back pressure drops.

</Think>

<Plan>

Define, then picture all three states (static, stagnation, sonic) on one bar chart.

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Mach number, static">

- **Mach number**: local flow speed divided by the local speed of sound, $M = V/c$.
- **Static conditions**: the actual $p$, $T$, $\rho$ of the gas, as measured by an instrument moving with it.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Stagnation and sonic">

- **Stagnation conditions**: the state the gas would reach if brought to rest **isentropically** ($T_0$, $p_0$, $\rho_0$). It exists at every point, whether or not the gas ever stops.
- **Sonic (critical) conditions**: the state the gas would have if accelerated or decelerated isentropically to $M = 1$ ($T^*$, $p^*$, $A^*$).

</Step>
<Step title="3. Choking">

The limiting condition where the **mass flow rate no longer increases** when the downstream pressure is lowered further, because the minimum area has reached $M = 1$.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

As above. The ratios that link the states: $T^*/T_0 = 0.8333$, $p^*/p_0 = 0.5283$, $\rho^*/\rho_0 = 0.6339$.

</Answer>

- Anthony's stagnation definition omitted "isentropically"; his choking definition was about velocity. The official one is about $\dot m$, and that is the one to remember.
- $A^*$ is a reference area: the throat that *would* pass the same $\dot m$ at M = 1.

</Traps>

<Problem id="PS2 P2" title="Stagnation temperature at Mach 1 and Mach 5" tags="T₀/T, energy">

<Statement>

For PS1 P7's vehicles (Yeager at −57 °C, Green at 30 °C), compute the stagnation temperature (a) at M = 1 and (b) at M = 5.

<Think>

One line: $T_0/T = 1 + 0.2M^2$. This comes from energy alone, so it holds on the nose even behind a bow shock.

$T$ in K → multiply by $T_0/T$.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

- (a) 259.2 K (Yeager), 363.6 K (Green)
- (b) 1296 K (Yeager), 1818 K (Green)

</Answer>

<Problem id="PS2 P3" title="Isentropic duct: 600 m/s, then 30 kPa downstream" tags="invariants, inverting p₀/p">

<Statement>

Air flows isentropically in a duct. At one section V = 600 m/s, T = 5 °C, p = 70 kPa. (a) Find M, T₀, p₀ there. (b) At another point p = 30 kPa: find M, T and V.

</Statement>

<Think>

Textbook invariants problem. Station 1 is already supersonic; the lower downstream pressure means further supersonic expansion (a diverging duct).

</Think>

<Plan>

$c_1$ → $M_1$ → $T_0$, $p_0$ → $p_0/p_2$ → $M_2$ → $T_2$ → $V_2$.

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Mach number at station 1">

$c_1 = \sqrt{1.4 \times 287.06 \times 278} = 334.2$ m/s, $M_1 = 600/334.2 = 1.795$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Invariants">

$T_0 = 278(1 + 0.2 \times 1.795^2) = 457.2$ K. $p_0 = 70 \times 1.6444^{3.5} = 399.2$ kPa. <T>A2 @ M = 1.80: p/p₀ = 0.1740, T/T₀ = 0.6068</T> gives 402 kPa and 458 K.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Mach number at station 2">

$p_0/p_2 = 399.2/30 = 13.31$:

$$
M_2 = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}\left[\left(\frac{p_0}{p_2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} - 1\right]} = \sqrt{5\,(13.31^{0.2857} - 1)} = 2.34
$$

<T>A2 @ p/p₀ = 0.0752: M = 2.34</T> — <CalcLink calc="isentropic" q="in=p&v=0.0752">check</CalcLink>

</Step>
<Step title="4. T₂ and V₂">

$T_2 = 457.2/(1 + 0.2 \times 2.34^2) = 218.2$ K; $V_2 = 2.34\sqrt{1.4 \times 287.06 \times 218.2} = 693$ m/s.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

- (a) $M_1 = 1.80$, $T_0 = 457$ K, $p_0 = 399$ kPa
- (b) $M_2 = 2.34$, $T_2 = 218$ K, $V_2 = 693$ m/s

</Answer>

<Traps>

- R = 287 vs 287.7 changes only the third digit (Anthony got 2.33 and 691 m/s).
- Lower pressure downstream in a supersonic flow means a *diverging* duct.

</Traps>

<Problem id="PS2 P4" title="CO₂ duct with γ = 1.3" tags="γ ≠ 1.4, no tables">

<Statement>

CO₂ (MW = 44, γ = 1.3) flows isentropically in a duct. At one point V = 200 m/s, T = 50 °C, p = 50 kPa. At another point p = 15 kPa. Find M and T there.

<Plan>

$R = 8314/44$ → $c_1$ → $M_1$ → $T_0$, $p_0$ → invert $p_0/p_2$ → $M_2$, $T_2$.

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. State 1">

$R = 188.95$ J/kg·K; $c_1 = \sqrt{1.3 \times 188.95 \times 323} = 281.7$ m/s; $M_1 = 200/281.7 = 0.710$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Invariants">

$T_0 = 323(1 + 0.15 \times 0.710^2) = 347.4$ K; $p_0 = 50(1.0756)^{4.333} = 68.57$ kPa.

$$

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$M_2 = 1.67$, $T_2 = 245$ K (−28.5 °C).

</Answer>

<Problem id="PS2 P5" title="N₂ tank through a converging nozzle" tags="converging nozzle, choking, Fliegner">

<Statement>

N₂ (MW 28, γ 1.4) in a large chamber at 450 K and 1.5 atm leaves through a converging nozzle with a 30 cm² outlet into 1 atm. Isentropic. Find (a) the exit velocity if $p_\text{exit}$ matches ambient, (b) the mass flow, (c) the maximum velocity and mass flow obtainable by lowering the ambient pressure.

</Statement>

<Think>

**First check for choking**: $p_\text{amb}/p_0 = 0.667 > 0.528$, so not choked and $p_\text{exit} = p_\text{amb}$ (the latex balloon). For (c) the limit is $M = 1$ at the exit (the Bell Centre door).

</Think>

<Plan>

(a) $p_0/p_e$ → $M_e$ → $T_e$ → $V_e$. (b) $\rho_e V_e A$. (c) Fliegner at M = 1, $T^* = 0.8333\,T_0$.

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Exit Mach (a)">

$p_0/p_e = 1.5$ ⇒ $M_e = \sqrt{5(1.5^{0.2857} - 1)} = 0.784$. <T>A2 @ M = 0.78: p/p₀ = 0.6690</T>

</Step>
<Step title="2. Exit velocity (a)">

$T_e = 450/(1 + 0.2 \times 0.784^2) = 400.8$ K; $c_e = \sqrt{1.4 \times 296.9 \times 400.8} = 408.2$ m/s; $V_e = 0.784 \times 408.2 = 319.9$ m/s.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Mass flow (b)">

$\rho_e = 101\,325/(296.9 \times 400.8) = 0.851$ kg/m³; $\dot m = 0.851 \times 319.9 \times 0.003 = 0.817$ kg/s.

</Step>
<Step title="4. Maximum (c)">

Choked exit: $T^* = 450 \times 0.8333 = 375$ K, $V_\text{max} = \sqrt{1.4 \times 296.9 \times 375} = 394.8$ m/s.

$$
\dot m_\text{max} = A\frac{p_0}{\sqrt{T_0}}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^3 = 0.003 \times \frac{151\,988}{\sqrt{450}} \times 0.0687 \times 0.5787 = 0.854\ \text{kg/s}
$$

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

- (a) $V_e = 320$ m/s ($M_e = 0.784$)
- (b) $\dot m = 0.817$ kg/s
- (c) $V_\text{max} = 395$ m/s at M = 1; $\dot m_\text{max} = 0.854$ kg/s, reached once $p_\text{amb} < 0.528 \times 1.5 = 0.792$ atm.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- A converging nozzle can never exceed M = 1, no matter how low the ambient pressure.
- The gain from choking is small: 0.817 → 0.854 kg/s (+4.5%), even if the ambient went to vacuum.
- Use the N₂ Fliegner constant (0.0397), not air's 0.0404.

</Traps>

<Problem id="PS2 P6" title="Aircraft inlet: M 0.95 to 0.3" tags="subsonic diffuser, A/A*">

<Statement>

An aircraft flies at M = 0.95 where p = 30 kPa and T = −50 °C. The inlet isentropically slows the air to M = 0.3 for the engine. Find p and T at the engine and the area ratio (inlet entrance)/(engine entrance).

</Statement>

<Think>

Subsonic diffuser: it must **diverge** to slow the flow. $T_0$, $p_0$, $A^*$ are constant; the area ratio is just a ratio of two $A/A^*$ readings.

</Think>

<Plan>

Invariants at 1 → $T_2$, $p_2$ at M = 0.3 → $A_1/A_2 = (A/A^*)_1/(A/A^*)_2$.

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Invariants">

$T_0 = 223 \times 1.1805 = 263.3$ K; $p_0 = 30 \times 1.1805^{3.5} = 53.62$ kPa.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Engine face">

$T_2 = 263.3/1.018 = 258.6$ K; $p_2 = 53.62 \times 0.9395 = 50.38$ kPa (<T>A2 @ M = 0.30: p/p₀ = 0.9395, T/T₀ = 0.9823</T>).

</Step>
<Step title="3. Area ratio">

<T>A2 @ M = 0.95: A/A* = 1.0021</T>, <T>A2 @ M = 0.30: A/A* = 2.0351</T>:

$$
\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{1.0021}{2.0351} = 0.492
$$

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$p_\text{engine} = 50.4$ kPa, $T_\text{engine} = 258.6$ K, $A_1/A_2 = 0.492$ (the passage roughly doubles in area).

</Answer>

<Problem id="PS2 P8" title="Suck-down Mach 4 wind tunnel" tags="reservoir = atmosphere, A/A*, test conditions">

<Statement>

A "suck-down" tunnel draws atmospheric air (300 K, 101 kPa, 1.226 kg/m³) through a nozzle into an evacuated tank. (a) For M = 4 in the test section, find T, p, ρ there. (b) Find $A_\text{test}/A_\text{throat}$. (c) It should simulate flight at 10 000 m (26 kPa, −50 °C). What are the disadvantages, and how could you fix them?

</Statement>

<Think>

The atmosphere is at rest, so **the atmosphere is the stagnation state**. Then it is one row of Table A2 at M = 4.

</Think>

<Plan>

M = 4 row → static state; A/A* → area ratio (choked throat, $A^* = A_t$); compare with flight.

</Plan>

</Step>

$$
\frac{A_\text{test}}{A_t} = \frac{1}{4}\left[\frac{2}{2.4}(4.2)\right]^3 = 10.72 \qquad \text{(A2: 10.719)}

</Step>

<Problem id="PS2 P10" title="The hidden throat in PS1 P2 (and why it isn't isentropic)" tags="A*, entropy check">

<Statement>

In PS1 P2 (N₂, 12 m/s, 14 atm, 800 K, A₁ = 0.05 m² → 590 K, A₂ = 0.15 m²), what was the minimum area between the entrance and the exit?

</Statement>

<Think>

Gas accelerated from 12 m/s to 680 m/s through a net-diverging device, so it must have passed a throat and gone supersonic. Isentropically, the throat is $A^*$, found at station 1. But check whether the flow really is isentropic: the problem gives more data than needed.

</Think>

<Plan>

$M_1$ → $A_1/A^*$ → $A^*$. Then $\Delta s$ between 1 and 2.

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. M₁">

$M_1 = 12/\sqrt{1.4 \times 296.9 \times 800} = 12/576.7 = 0.0208$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. A*">

$A_1/A^* = 27.83$ (formula; off the table), so $A^* = 0.05/27.83 = 0.00180$ m² = 18 cm². Anthony's route via Fliegner gives the same: $\dot m = 3.58$ kg/s, $A^* = 1.80\times10^{-3}$ m².

</Step>
<Step title="3. Entropy check">

With $c_p = 1100$: $V_2 = 679.8$ m/s, $p_2 = 0.0608$ atm, and

$$
\Delta s = 1100\ln\frac{590}{800} - 296.9\ln\frac{0.0608}{14} = -334 + 1615 = +1281\ \text{J/kg·K}
$$

Entropy rose: **not isentropic**.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$A_\text{min} = A^* = 0.0018$ m² (18 cm²) if isentropic. Because $\Delta s = +1281$ J/kg·K, irreversibilities lower $p_0$ and **enlarge** $A^*$, so the real throat may be larger.

</Answer>

<DuctSetupFigure M1={0.0208} M2={1.373} left={['1: 0.05 m²', 'M = 0.021']} right={['2: 0.15 m²', 'M = 1.37']} middle="throat ≥ A* = 18 cm² (isentropic estimate)" caption="PS2 P10: the device must contain a converging–diverging passage. <Traps>

<Problem id="PS2 P11" title="Derive A/A* from the velocity–area relation" tags="derivation, log differentiation">

<Statement>

Starting from $\frac{dV}{V} = \frac{1}{M^2-1}\frac{dA}{A}$, express $V$ in terms of $M$ (use $V = Mc$ and $dh + V\,dV = 0$) to get $\frac{dV}{V} = \frac{1}{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2}\frac{dM}{M}$. Combine, integrate between $A^*$ ($M = 1$) and $A$ ($M$), and recover the $A/A^*$ relation.

</Statement>

<Think>

A derivation: logarithmic differentiation ($d\ln x = dx/x$) turns products into sums, then a partial-fraction integral.

</Think>

<Plan>

$dV/V$ in terms of $dM/M$ → $dA/A$ as a function of $M$ times $dM/M$ → integrate → exponentiate.

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. V = Mc, log-differentiate">

$\frac{dV}{V} = \frac{dM}{M} + \frac{dc}{c}$ and $c = \sqrt{\gamma RT}$ gives $\frac{dc}{c} = \frac12\frac{dT}{T}$.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Energy">

$c_p dT = -V dV$ ⇒ $\frac{dT}{T} = -\frac{V^2}{c_pT}\frac{dV}{V} = -(\gamma-1)M^2\frac{dV}{V}$ (using $V^2 = M^2\gamma RT$ and $c_p = \gamma R/(\gamma-1)$).

</Step>
<Step title="3. Isolate dV/V">

$\frac{dV}{V} = \frac{dM}{M} - \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\frac{dV}{V}$ ⇒ $\frac{dV}{V} = \frac{1}{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2}\frac{dM}{M}$

</Step>
<Step title="4. Combine with the area rule">

$$
\frac{dA}{A} = \frac{M^2 - 1}{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2}\frac{dM}{M}
$$

</Step>
<Step title="5. Partial fractions (k = (γ−1)/2)">

$$
\frac{M^2-1}{M(1+kM^2)} = -\frac{1}{M} + \frac{(1+k)M}{1+kM^2}
\;\Rightarrow\;
\int = -\ln M + \frac{1+k}{2k}\ln(1+kM^2), \qquad \frac{1+k}{2k} = \frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}
$$

</Step>
<Step title="6. Limits A* (M = 1) to A (M)">

$$
\ln\frac{A}{A^*} = -\ln M + \frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}\ln\frac{1+kM^2}{1+k}
\;\Rightarrow\;
\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{M}\left[\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\left(1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\right)\right]^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}
$$

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

$\frac{dM}{M} = \frac{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2}{M^2-1}\frac{dA}{A}$, which integrates to the familiar $A/A^*$ relation.

</Answer>
