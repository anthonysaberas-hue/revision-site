<Problem id="F2023 P1" title="Channel with two throats: the blocked wind tunnel (50 pts: a 15, b 20, c 15)" tags="choking, normal shock, second throat, wind tunnel">

<Statement>

A tank of quiescent air (MW = 30 kg/kmol) at 10 bar and 300 K feeds a channel with a cross-sectional area of 5 cm². The channel has two throats, $A_{t1}$ = 1 cm² and $A_{t2}$ = 1.5 cm², with an intermediate section between them at the original channel area (5 cm²). The channel exhausts into a tank with back pressure $p_{back}$ = 5 bar. Neglect friction.

(a) Find the mass flow rate through the channel in kg/s.

(b) Find the static pressure (in bar) in the intermediate section between the two throats.

(c) Redraw the sketch, label the state of the flow in each region (subsonic, supersonic or sonic), and indicate the location of any shock waves.

</Statement>

<Think>

Two throats in series means **a supersonic wind tunnel with a diffuser** ([Ch. 9](#/mech430/ch/inlets)). Three decisions, in order:

1. **Does it choke?** $p_b = 5$ bar is below $p^* = 0.5283 \times 10 = 5.283$ bar, so yes. It chokes at the **smaller** throat, $A_{t1}$.
2. **What happens after $t_1$?** Neither a subsonic nor a fully supersonic solution matches 5 bar, so there is a shock.
3. **How strong is the shock?** The flow behind it has to fit through $t_2$. Take $A^*_y = A_{t2}$. Then $p_{0y}/p_{0x} = A^*_x/A^*_y = A_{t1}/A_{t2}$ fixes the shock **without any iteration**.

</Think>

<Plan>

tank → contraction → $t_1$ (M = 1) → supersonic → **shock 1** → subsonic intermediate section → $t_2$ (M = 1) → supersonic → **shock 2** → subsonic → 5 bar.

<TwoThroatSection3D />

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. (a) Choked at the first throat">

$p_b = 5\text{ bar} < p^* = 0.5283\,p_0 = 5.283$ bar, so the flow **must** choke, and it chokes at the minimum area, $A_{t1} = 1$ cm²:

$$
\dot m = \frac{p_0}{\sqrt{T_0}}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}}\,\frac{A_{t1}}{1.2^{3}} = \frac{10^6}{\sqrt{300}}\sqrt{\frac{1.4}{277.1}}\times\frac{10^{-4}}{1.728} = 0.237\ \text{kg/s}
$$

<CalcLink calc="isentropic" q="in=M&v=1">sonic ratios</CalcLink>

</Step>
<Step title="2. (b) Rule out the shock-free solutions">

Subsonic after $t_1$, all the way to the exit:

<T>A2 @ A/A* = 5 (sub): M = 0.12, p/p₀ = 0.98998</T> → 9.90 bar ≠ 5 bar. Not valid. — <CalcLink calc="isentropic" q="in=A&v=5&b=sub">check</CalcLink>

Supersonic and isentropic after $t_1$:

<T>A2 @ A/A* = 5 (sup): M = 3.2, p/p₀ = 0.02023</T> → 0.20 bar, far below 5 bar. So there is a shock. — <CalcLink calc="isentropic" q="in=A&v=5&b=sup">check</CalcLink>

</Step>
<Step title="3. (b) The shock strength from the second throat">

After the shock $A^*_y$ is larger than $A_{t1}$, but it cannot be larger than $A_{t2}$ (the flow must pass $t_2$). Take $A^*_y = A_{t2}$, so the second throat is also choked:

$$
\frac{p_{0y}}{p_{0x}} = \frac{A^*_x}{A^*_y} = \frac{1}{1.5} = 0.667
$$

<T>A3 @ p₀ᵧ/p₀ₓ = 0.6666: Mₓ = 2.11</T> (exact 2.116), so $p_{0y} = 6.67$ bar — <CalcLink calc="normal" q="in=p0&v=0.6667">check</CalcLink>

Check: $M_x = 2.11$ is less than the 3.2 reached at the end of the diverging section, so the shock sits **inside the diverging part after $t_1$**, at $A = 1.86$ cm².

</Step>
<Step title="4. (b) Intermediate-section pressure">

$A/A^*_y = 5/1.5 = 3.33$, subsonic:

<T>A2 @ A/A* = 3.33 (sub): M = 0.18, p/p₀ = 0.97765</T> (exact M = 0.177) — <CalcLink calc="isentropic" q="in=A&v=3.333&b=sub">check</CalcLink>

$$
p_{inter} = 0.97765 \times 6.67 = 6.5\ \text{bar}
$$

The official solution read the M = 0.2 row ($p/p_0 = 0.9725$, giving 6.48 bar).

</Step>
<Step title="5. (c) After the second throat: a second shock">

The intermediate flow reaches $t_2$ at M = 1. After it:
- Subsonic: $p_{exit} = 6.5$ bar ≠ 5 bar.
- Supersonic: <T>A2 @ A/A* = 3.33 (sup): M = 2.75, p/p₀ = 0.0398</T> → 0.26 bar ≠ 5 bar.

So the flow goes supersonic after $t_2$ and then **shocks in the diverging part after $t_2$**. Its exact position needs iteration. The lib puts it at $A \approx 2.29$ cm² with $M_x \approx 1.88$, but the quiz only asks you to show that it is there.

<TwoThroatTunnelFigure />

</Step>
<Step title="6. (c) Why not supersonic through both throats?">

The official solution also considers flow that stays supersonic through $t_2$ (M = 1.85 there), with a single shock further downstream. That state exists mathematically, but **it cannot be reached with fixed areas**. To get there, shock 1 would have to be swallowed through $t_2$, which needs $A_{t2}/A_{t1} \geq p_{0x}/p_{0y}$ at the test-section Mach number (about 3.2), roughly 3.5, not 1.5. This is the "blocked" wind tunnel of the MIT film *Channel Flow of a Compressible Fluid*. Toggle "Started" in the 3-D section above to see the unreachable state.

<TwoThroatTunnelFigure scenario="started" />

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

- (a) **ṁ = 0.237 kg/s** (choked at $t_1$).
- (b) **$p_{inter} \approx 6.5$ bar** with $M \approx 0.18$. The official value is 6.48 bar, from the M = 0.2 row.
- (c) ① M < 1 → ② M = 1 at $t_1$ → ③ M > 1 → **shock 1** ($M_x$ = 2.11, diverging part after $t_1$) → ④ M < 1 → ⑤ M = 1 at $t_2$ → ⑥ M > 1 → **shock 2** (diverging part after $t_2$) → ⑦ M < 1 to 5 bar.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- **Official slip, corrected:** for $A/A^* = 3.33$ the official reads M = 0.2, but that row has $A/A^* = 2.96$. The nearest row is **M = 0.18** ($A/A^*$ = 3.28), which gives **6.5 bar**. The difference is small (6.48 vs 6.5 bar), and both were accepted.
- The flow chokes at the **smaller** throat. The key closure is $A^*_y = A_{t2}$.
- The drawing was worth 15 pts: 2 for each shock and 2 for its position (8 in total), and 7 for the M < 1 / = 1 / > 1 labels. Anthony's note to himself: **"Be aware of location!!"** Both shocks belong in the **diverging** parts.
- **Exam-speed version:** "a) $p_b$ = 5 < 5.283 ⇒ choke at $t_1$ (min A): ṁ = 0.237. b) sub: A/A* = 5 → 9.9 bar ≠ 5; sup: M = 3.2 → 0.20 bar ≠ 5 ⇒ shock. Take $A^*_y$ = $A_{t2}$ = 1.5 → $p_{0y}/p_{0x}$ = 0.667 → $M_x$ = 2.11 (< 3.2 ⇒ in the diverging part). A/A* = 3.33 → M = 0.18 → p = 0.97765 × 6.67 = 6.5 bar. c) at $t_2$: 6.5 ≠ 5 and 0.26 ≠ 5 ⇒ sonic, supersonic, second shock in the diverging part."

</Traps>

<Problem id="F2023 P2" title="Tank feeding a long friction duct (50 pts: a 30, b 10, c 10)" tags="Fanno, choked exit, mass flow">

<Statement>

A tank of quiescent air (MW = 30 kg/kmol) at 10 bar and 300 K feeds a channel with a square 1 cm × 1 cm cross-section, so the hydraulic diameter is $D_H$ = 1 cm. The channel is 10 m long with a friction coefficient f = 0.005. It exhausts into a chamber with back pressure $p_{back}$ = 1 bar.

(a) What is the Mach number at the entrance of the channel?

(b) What is the Mach number at the exit of the channel?

(c) What is the mass flow rate in kg/s?

</Statement>

<Think>

There is no throat and no diverging section, so the flow entering the duct is **subsonic**. The duct is very long ($4fL/D = 20$), so friction alone will choke it at the exit (recipe R4).

Anthony's decision rule: "Since we don't know the state, **assume L = L***." That gives $M_1$ straight from A4. Then check whether $p^* > p_b$. If it is, the exit is choked, and the exit pressure does not have to match $p_b$.

</Think>

<Plan>

$4fL/D$ → (assume $L = L^*$) → A4 → $M_1$ → A2 → $p_1$ → A4 → $p^*$ → compare with $p_b$ → ṁ from the isentropic mass-flux formula at $M_1$, using the duct area.

<TankDuctSection3D fL={20} />

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Friction length">

$$
\frac{4fL}{D_H} = \frac{4(0.005)(10)}{0.01} = 20
$$

</Step>
<Step title="2. (a) Assume the duct is exactly L* long">

<T>A4 @ 4fL*/D = 20 (sub): M₁ = 0.17, p/p* = 6.4252</T> (exact M₁ = 0.174) — <CalcLink calc="fanno" q="in=fL&v=20&b=sub">check</CalcLink>

<T>A2 @ M = 0.17: p/p₀ = 0.98003</T> → $p_1 = 9.80$ bar — <CalcLink calc="isentropic" q="in=M&v=0.17">check</CalcLink>

$$
p^* = p_2 = \frac{9.8003}{6.4252} = 1.525\ \text{bar}
$$

</Step>
<Step title="3. (a) Is the choked solution the right one?">

$p^* = 1.525$ bar is **above** $p_b = 1$ bar. Once the back pressure is below $p^*$, the duct is isolated from it, so $p_{exit} = p^*$ need not match $p_b$ (+5 pts on the marking).

Optional check (the official "certain it chokes" step): try $M_1 = 0.15$.

<T>A4 @ M = 0.15: 4fL*/D = 27.932, p/p* = 7.2886</T> → $27.932 - 20 = 7.932$ → <T>A4: M₂ = 0.26, p/p* = 4.185</T> → $p_2 = 5.65$ bar

That exit is subsonic, so it must equal $p_b$, and it doesn't. Every subsonic solution has $p_{exit} > 1.525 > 1$ bar, and any $M_1 > 0.17$ would need $L > L^*$. **The only solution is the choked one.**

<TankDuctExitPlot fL={20} p0={10} pb={1} guesses={[0.15]} />

</Step>
<Step title="4. (b) Exit Mach number">

The duct is exactly $L^*$ long: **$M_2 = 1$**, with $p_{exit} = 1.525$ bar, which is greater than $p_b$.

</Step>
<Step title="5. (c) Mass flow">

Use the isentropic mass-flux formula with the **entrance** Mach number and the duct area (the duct is constant-area; M = 1 occurs only at its exit):

$$
\dot m = A\,\frac{p_0}{\sqrt{T_0}}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}}\;\frac{M_1}{(1 + 0.2M_1^2)^3} = 10^{-4}\times\frac{10^6}{\sqrt{300}}\sqrt{\frac{1.4}{277.1}}\times\frac{0.17}{(1.00578)^3} = 0.0686\ \text{kg/s}
$$

With the exact $M_1 = 0.174$: 0.0702 kg/s. The official answer is "0.07 kg/s".

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

- (a) **$M_1 \approx 0.17$** (exact 0.174).
- (b) **$M_2 = 1$**: choked at the exit, with $p_{exit} = p^* \approx 1.53$ bar, above $p_b$ = 1 bar.
- (c) **ṁ ≈ 0.07 kg/s** (0.0686 with M = 0.17).

</Answer>

<Traps>

- The solution sheet writes "$P_0$ = 10 atm" and "$P_{back}$ = 1 atm". The question uses **bar**.
- Once choked, $p_{exit} = p^* \neq p_b$. Realising this was worth 5 pts.
- ṁ uses the **entrance** Mach number (0.17) in the isentropic formula, not M = 1.
- The red marking allowed either iterating on $M_1$ or "jumping directly to the answer with $L^* = L$". The direct jump is faster.
- **Exam-speed version:** "a) no exit condition ⇒ assume L = L*: 4fL/D = 20 → $M_1$ = 0.17. p = 0.98 × 10 = 9.8, p* = 1.52 > $p_b$ ⇒ choked (any M > 0.17 needs L > L*; any lower M gives $p_{exit}$ > p* > $p_b$). b) $M_2$ = 1. c) ṁ = A($p_0/\sqrt{T_0}$)$\sqrt{\gamma/R}$ M/(1 + 0.2M²)³ = 0.0686 ≈ 0.07 kg/s."

</Traps>
