<Problem id="Final B P1" title="Supersonic inlet: design area ratio, starting Mach, unstarted mass flow (20 pts)" tags="inlet, normal shock, Kantrowitz, spillage">

<Statement>

A supersonic inlet is designed to decelerate an incoming flow of air at **M∞ = 1.4** isentropically (scenario a).

(a) Find the **inlet-to-throat area ratio** for the isentropic solution. This ratio stays fixed for the rest of the problem.

(b) To what value must the **incoming flow Mach number** be increased to *start* the inlet by forcing a normal shock into the inlet (scenario b)?

(c) Before the inlet is started, a normal shock stands in front of the inlet and some of the approaching air is diverted around it. For **M∞ = 1.4**, what is the **ratio of the mass flow rate** through the unstarted inlet (c) to the mass flow when the inlet is started at the same Mach number (a)?

</Statement>

<Think>

The [inlet-starting](#/mech430/ch/inlets) family, same as [W2019 P1](#/mech430/exam/q2-w2019#w2019-p1) and [W2023 P1](#/mech430/exam/q2-w2023#w2023-p1). Three one-liners:

- design: $A_{in}/A_t = A/A^*(M_d)$;
- start by overspeeding: the shock must sit at the lip *and* the post-shock flow must still squeeze through the throat, so $A_{in}/A_t = A/A^*(M_y)$ on the **subsonic** branch;
- unstarted: the throat still chokes, but on the post-shock $p_0$, so $\dot m \propto p_0$ and the ratio is $p_{0y}/p_{0x}$.

</Think>

<Plan>

(a) A2 at 1.4. (b) A2 subsonic at the same ratio → $M_y$ → A3 inverse → $M_x$. (c) A3 at 1.4: $p_{0y}/p_{0x}$.

<InletPanelsFigure />

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. (a) Design ratio">

<T>A2 @ M = 1.4: A/A* = 1.1149</T>. So $A_{in}/A_t = 1.1149$. — <CalcLink calc="isentropic" q="in=M&v=1.4">check</CalcLink>

</Step>
<Step title="2. (b) Starting Mach">

With the shock at the lip, the throat is the sonic throat of the **post-shock** flow: <T>A2 (sub) @ A/A* = 1.1149: M_y = 0.67</T> (exact 0.6735). Then <T>A3 @ M_y = 0.67: Mₓ = 1.59</T> (exact 1.583). — <CalcLink calc="normal" q="in=My&v=0.6735">check</CalcLink>

</Step>
<Step title="3. (c) Unstarted mass flow">

$\dot m = A_t \dfrac{p_0}{\sqrt{T_0}}\sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma}{R}}\,\dfrac{1}{1.2^3}$ at the choked throat. Same throat, same $T_0$, M = 1 in both cases: only $p_0$ changes. <T>A3 @ 1.4: p₀y/p₀ₓ = 0.9582</T>.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

- (a) $A_{in}/A_t = 1.115$.
- (b) $M_\infty = 1.59$ (exact 1.583).
- (c) $\dot m_{unstarted}/\dot m_{started} = 0.958$.
- Both students are correct to table precision.

</Answer>

<Problem id="Final B P2" title="Tank to vacuum through a long capillary: Fanno choking; halve the length for twice the flow (20 pts)" tags="Fanno, choking, mass flow">

<Statement>

A tank of air (MW = 28.9 kg/kmol) at **1000 kPa and 300 K** is connected to a downstream tank held at near-perfect vacuum ($p_{back} \approx 0$) by a vacuum pump. Because of the low back pressure, the flow in the tube will always choke. The connection is a **10 m** long tube of diameter **D_H = 1 mm** with friction coefficient **f = 0.005**.

(a) Compute the **mass flow rate** of air from the tank **[g/s]**. (Remember to convert the tube diameter into cross-sectional area.)

(b) To **double** the mass flow rate with the same reservoir and back conditions, the tube must be **shortened to what length [m]**?

</Statement>

<Think>

[Fanno](#/mech430/ch/fanno) with a vacuum downstream: the exit is sonic, so the **whole tube is one $L^*$**. The tube length therefore fixes the inlet Mach number, and the isentropic entry from the tank fixes $\dot m$. This is Anthony's recipe R4 ([F2023 P2](#/mech430/exam/q2-f2023#f2023-p2) is its twin).

</Think>

<Plan>

$4fL/D$ → A4 (sub) → $M_{in}$ → $\dot m = A\frac{p_0}{\sqrt{T_0}}\sqrt{\gamma/R}\,M/(1+0.2M^2)^3$. For (b): double $M/(1+0.2M^2)^3$ → new $M_{in}$ → A4 → new $L$.

<VacuumTubeSim />

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. (a) Inlet Mach from the tube length">

$4fL/D = 4(0.005)(10)/10^{-3} = 200 = 4fL^*/D$. <T>A4 @ 4fL*/D ≈ 200: M = 0.06</T> (the table row 0.06 reads 193.0; exact $M_{in} = 0.0590$). — <CalcLink calc="fanno" q="in=fL&v=200&b=sub">check</CalcLink>

</Step>
<Step title="2. (a) Mass flow">

$A = \pi(10^{-3})^2/4 = 7.854 \times 10^{-7}$ m², $R = 287.7$ J/kg·K.

$$
\dot m = 7.854\times10^{-7}\,\frac{10^6}{\sqrt{300}}\sqrt{\frac{1.4}{287.7}}\;\frac{0.06}{(1+0.2\times0.06^2)^3} = 1.894\times10^{-4}\ \text{kg/s} = 0.189\ \text{g/s}
$$

(With the exact $M_{in} = 0.0590$: 0.186 g/s.)

</Step>
<Step title="3. (b) New inlet Mach for twice the flow">

$\dot m \propto M/(1+0.2M^2)^3$, nearly linear at low M. Solve $M/(1+0.2M^2)^3 = 2 \times 0.05987 = 0.1197$: $M_{in} = 0.1208$.

</Step>
<Step title="4. (b) New length">

<T>A4 @ M = 0.12: 4fL*/D = 45.408</T> → $L = 45.408 \times 10^{-3}/(4 \times 0.005) = 2.27$ m.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

- (a) **ṁ ≈ 0.189 g/s** (1.89 × 10⁻⁴ kg/s), with $M_{in} \approx 0.06$.
- (b) **L ≈ 2.27 m** (exact chain 2.32 m; the difference is the table rounding of $M_{in}$).
- Corrections: both students wrote **45.508**, but their own L = 2.2704 m comes from the correct table value **45.408**. The alternate student's "1.89 kg/s" is a units error: **1.89 × 10⁻⁴ kg/s**. Their "shock at exit" wording is also wrong: the exit is sonic, and there is no shock.

</Answer>

<Traps>

- Convert D = 1 mm to $A = \pi D^2/4$, and answer in **g/s**.
- ṁ uses the **tube-inlet** Mach number with the tube area, not M = 1.
- Halving the length does not double the flow: you need about a quarter of the friction length (200 → 45) because $4fL^*/D \approx 1/(\gamma M^2)$ at low M.

</Traps>

<Problem id="Final B P3" title="Ramjet: inlet shock, Rayleigh combustor, nozzle sizing, thrust, over-fuelling (20 pts)" tags="ramjet, normal shock, Rayleigh, thrust, unstart">

<Statement>

A supersonic ramjet is designed to fly at **Mach 3**, where the approaching air is at **T∞ = 250 K, p∞ = 10 kPa**. The inlet area is **1 m²**, and the shock wave sits where **A_shock = 0.4 m²**. After the shock the flow expands to **A_combustor = 0.6 m²**, where **Δq = 500 kJ/kg** is added by combustion. Use **c_p = 1 kJ/kg·K** and **MW_air = 28.9 kg/kmol**. Finally a nozzle expands the heated flow to generate thrust.

(a) If the exit nozzle isentropically expands the flow to match the ambient pressure $p_\infty = 10$ kPa, what are the **throat area $A_{throat}$ and exit area $A_{exit}$** of the nozzle [m²]?

(b) Enclosing the entire ramjet in a control volume, compute the **thrust** [N].

(c) If the pilot increases the heat addition to **1000 kJ/kg** by increasing the fuel rate, describe **what happens to the operation of the engine**. (Detailed calculations not required.)

*Figure: inlet (1 m²) converging to an inlet throat (M = 1), supersonic, a normal shock at 0.4 m², subsonic diffuser to the combustor (0.6 m², flame Δq), converging to the nozzle throat and diverging to the exit; a dashed control volume encloses it all.*

</Statement>

<Think>

The [combined-engine recipe](#/mech430/page/recipes#7-combined-engine-ramjet): march station by station and hand on M, $p_0$, $T_0$ at every junction.

- inlet: isentropic to $M = 1$, so $A_t^* = A_{in}/(A/A^*)(3)$;
- shock: position fixes $M_x$ via $A_{shock}/A^*$; $p_0$ drops, $T_0$ stays, $A^*$ grows;
- diffuser: isentropic subsonic with the new $A^*_y$;
- combustor: [Rayleigh](#/mech430/ch/rayleigh), $T_0$ rises by Δq/c_p, $p_0$ falls (Table **A-5**);
- nozzle: choked throat, then expand to $p_\infty$.

This is where both students tripped: in the Rayleigh step **$p_0/p_0^*$ comes from A-5, not A-2**.

</Think>

<Plan>

$M_\infty$ → $A^*_x$ → $M_x$ → $p_{0y}$, $A^*_y$ → $M_{c}$ → (A-5) $T_0^*$, $p_0^*$ → $T_{02}$ → $M_2$ → $p_{02}$ → $A_t = A_c/(A/A^*)(M_2)$ → $M_e$ from $p_{02}/p_\infty$ → $A_e$ → thrust.

<RamjetSim preset="finalB" />

</Plan>

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Inlet throat and free-stream stagnation state">

<T>A2 @ M = 3: A/A* = 4.2346, p/p₀ = 0.02722, T/T₀ = 0.3571</T> → $A^*_x = 1/4.2346 = 0.2361$ m², $p_{0x} = 10/0.02722 = 367.4$ kPa, $T_0 = 700$ K.

</Step>
<Step title="2. Shock strength from its position">

$A_{shock}/A^*_x = 0.4/0.2361 = 1.694$ → <T>A2 (sup) @ 1.69: M = 2.0</T> (exact 2.004). <T>A3 @ Mₓ = 2: M_y = 0.5774, p₀y/p₀ₓ = 0.7209</T> → $p_{0y} = 264.8$ kPa, $T_{0y} = 700$ K.

</Step>
<Step title="3. Diffuser to the combustor">

$A^*_y = A^*_x/0.7209 = 0.3276$ m². $A_c/A^*_y = 0.6/0.3276 = 1.832$ → <T>A2 (sub) @ 1.83: M = 0.34</T> (exact 0.339).

</Step>
<Step title="4. Rayleigh combustor">

$T_{02} = 700 + 500/1 = 1200$ K. <T>A5 @ M = 0.34: T₀/T₀* = 0.4206, p₀/p₀* = 1.1822</T> → $T_0^* = 1664$ K, $p_0^* = 264.8/1.1822 = 224.0$ kPa.

Check choking: $\Delta q^* = c_p(T_0^* - T_{01}) = 964$ kJ/kg > 500 ✓.

$T_{02}/T_0^* = 1200/1664 = 0.721$ → <T>A5 (sub) @ T₀/T₀* = 0.72: M = 0.52, p₀/p₀* = 1.1059</T> → $p_{02} = 224.0 \times 1.1059 = 247.7$ kPa. — <CalcLink calc="rayleigh" q="in=M&v=0.34&b=sub">check</CalcLink>

<Trap title="The error both students made">

They used **1.8229**, the isentropic $A/A^*$ at M = 0.34, as if it were $p_0/p_0^*$ (and 1.3034, the A/A\* at 0.52, at the exit). That gave $p_{02}$ = 189 kPa (main) and 167 kPa (alternate) instead of 248 kPa. Both columns sit side by side in the printed tables; read the column header.

</Trap>

</Step>
<Step title="5. (a) Nozzle throat">

<T>A2 @ M = 0.52: A/A* = 1.3034</T> → $A_{throat} = 0.6/1.3034 = 0.460$ m² (both students got this right).

</Step>
<Step title="6. (a) Nozzle exit">

$p_\infty/p_{02} = 10/247.7 = 0.0404$ → <T>A2 @ p/p₀ = 0.0404: M = 2.74, A/A* = 3.31</T> → $A_{exit} = 3.31 \times 0.460 = 1.52$ m². — <CalcLink calc="isentropic" q="in=p&v=0.0404&b=sup">check</CalcLink>

</Step>
<Step title="7. (b) Thrust from a control volume around the engine">

The inlet is started, so it captures its full stream tube: $V_\infty = 3\sqrt{1.4 \times 287.7 \times 250} = 951.9$ m/s, $\rho_\infty = 10^4/(287.7 \times 250) = 0.139$ kg/m³, $\dot m = \rho_\infty V_\infty A_{in} = 132.4$ kg/s.

Exit: $T_e = 1200/(1 + 0.2 \times 2.74^2) = 480$ K, $V_e = 2.74\sqrt{1.4 \times 287.7 \times 480} = 1204$ m/s. With $p_e = p_\infty$ the pressure term vanishes and, neglecting the fuel mass:

$$
F = \dot m(V_e - V_\infty) + (p_e - p_\infty)A_e = 132.4 \times (1204 - 952) \approx 3.3\times10^4\ \text{N}
$$

</Step>
<Step title="8. (c) What over-fuelling does">

**Quick exam argument.** At the design combustor-entry Mach (0.34) the most heat the combustor can take is $\Delta q^* \approx 964$ kJ/kg (exact 970). Asking for 1000 kJ/kg would **thermally choke** it, so the flow upstream must change: the combustor-entry Mach must drop. With the fixed nozzle throat (0.46 m²), the extra heat also needs a higher $p_{02}$ (the choked mass flow $\dot m \propto p_{02}A_t/\sqrt{T_{02}}$ is fixed). The only way to get both is for the **normal shock to move upstream** toward the inlet throat, where it is weaker. Fuel it hard enough and the shock is pushed out of the throat: the inlet **unstarts**, a bow shock stands ahead, air spills and thrust collapses.

**What the full fixed-geometry calculation says** (the sim above, lib/ramjet.ts): at 1000 kJ/kg this particular engine survives. The shock moves forward from 0.40 m² to 0.31 m² ($M_x$ 2.00 → 1.65), the combustor-entry Mach drops to 0.27 and $p_{02}$ rises to 294 kPa. The unstart comes at about **1470 kJ/kg**, when the shock reaches the inlet throat. The main student's argument ("A\* required becomes larger than the exit A\* we have") is the right mechanism, but "a normal shock will be created" should read "the existing shock is pushed upstream, towards unstart".

</Step>
</Steps>

<Answer>

| Station | M | p₀ (kPa) | T₀ (K) | A (m²) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| free stream | 3 | 367.4 | 700 | 1 |
| inlet throat | 1 | 367.4 | 700 | 0.236 |
| shock x → y | 2.00 → 0.577 | 367.4 → 264.8 | 700 | 0.4 |
| combustor in | 0.34 | 264.8 | 700 | 0.6 |
| combustor out | 0.52 | 247.7 | 1200 | 0.6 |
| nozzle throat | 1 | 247.7 | 1200 | **0.46** |
| exit | 2.74 | 247.7 | 1200 | **1.52** |

- (a) $A_{throat} \approx 0.46$ m², **$A_{exit} \approx 1.52$ m²**. The students' 1.296 m² and 1.15 m² are wrong (A-2 used instead of A-5).
- (b) **F ≈ 33 kN** (digest calculation; neither student finished it).
- (c) The extra heat exceeds Δq\* at the design entry state, so the shock is pushed upstream (weaker, toward the throat). Too much fuel expels it: the inlet unstarts, spills air and loses thrust. For this geometry the limit is about 1470 kJ/kg.

</Answer>
