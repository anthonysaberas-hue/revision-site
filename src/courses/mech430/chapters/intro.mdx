# The big picture

<p className="lede">Compressible flow is what happens when a gas moves fast enough that its density changes noticeably. From about Mach 0.3 upward, pressure, density and temperature all move together with velocity — and past Mach 1 the rules flip.</p>

## Why this course exists

In Fluids 1 you treated density as a constant. That's fine for water, and for air below ~100 m/s. But a rocket nozzle, a jet inlet, a gas pipeline or a shock tube all move gas at speeds where

- the **density changes** (so continuity involves $\rho$, not just $V A$),
- the **temperature changes** (kinetic energy trades with enthalpy, $h + V^2/2 = $ const),
- **information travels at a finite speed** — the speed of sound $c$. Once the flow outruns $c$, downstream can no longer "talk" to upstream. That single fact causes choking, shocks, and every surprise in this course.

## Notation you'll see everywhere

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $0$ subscript | **stagnation** (total) state: the gas brought to rest isentropically — $T_0$, $p_0$, $\rho_0$ |
| $^*$ superscript | **sonic** reference state (where $M = 1$) — $A^*$, $p^*$, $T^*$ |
| $x$, $y$ | just **upstream** and **downstream** of a shock |
| $\sigma$, $\delta$ | oblique shock angle, flow deflection angle (Higgins' notation; textbooks use $\beta$, $\theta$) |
| $\nu$, $\mu$ | Prandtl–Meyer function, Mach angle |
| $V'$ (prime) | a velocity measured in the **lab frame** (for moving shocks) |
| $4fL/D$ | friction length with the **Fanning** friction factor $f$ |
