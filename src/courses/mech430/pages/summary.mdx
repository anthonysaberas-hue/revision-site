## The 7 ideas everything else follows from

1. **Information travels at the speed of sound, $c=\sqrt{\gamma R T}$.** When the flow is faster than $c$ ($M>1$), downstream can't talk to upstream. That one fact explains choking, shocks, and why a supersonic exit "can't feel" the back pressure.
2. **Energy: $h + V^2/2 = c_pT_0$ is constant in adiabatic flow.** Speed is bought with temperature. $T_0$ never changes unless heat is added or removed (Rayleigh).
3. **Losses show up as a drop in $p_0$.** Shocks and friction create entropy, $\Delta s = -R\ln(p_{02}/p_{01})$, so $p_0$ falls. In isentropic flow $p_0$, $T_0$ and $A^*$ are **invariants**: compute them once, reuse them everywhere.
4. **Area change flips sign at $M=1$:** $\dfrac{dV}{V}=\dfrac{1}{M^2-1}\dfrac{dA}{A}$. Subsonic flow speeds up in a converging duct; supersonic flow speeds up in a diverging duct. So you need a **throat** to go supersonic, and $M=1$ can only happen there.
5. **Choking.** Mass flux per area peaks at $M=1$, so a throat passes at most $\dot m = 0.0404\,\dfrac{p_0A^*}{\sqrt{T_0}}$ (air, SI). Once choked, lowering $p_b$ doesn't pull more mass: the Bell Centre doors are already at full throughput.
6. **Friction and heating both drive $M\to1$, from either side** (like a converging duct). Push past the limit and the flow chokes: subsonic flow lowers $\dot m$, supersonic flow puts a shock in.
7. **Supersonic flow turns through waves.** Turning into itself gives an oblique **shock** ($p$ up, $p_0$ lost). Turning away gives a Prandtl–Meyer **fan** ($p$ down, isentropic). A supersonic stream doesn't sense a pressure mismatch until it meets a wave.

## The decision tree for any duct problem

1. **Name the stations** (0 reservoir, t throat, x|y across a shock, e exit) and the three invariants $T_0$, $p_0$, $A^*$.
2. **Is it choked?** A converging nozzle chokes if $p_b \le 0.528\,p_0$. A C-D nozzle chokes if $p_b \le p_3$ (the subsonic-exit, sonic-throat solution). If choked, $\dot m$ comes from the throat, and $M_t=1$.
3. **Subsonic exit ⇒ $p_e = p_b$** (the latex balloon). **Supersonic exit ⇒ $p_e$ is whatever the nozzle gives**, and waves outside fix the mismatch.
4. **Where is the shock?** Guess its position, cross it with Table A3 ($T_0$ constant, new $p_{0y}$ and $A^*_y = A^*_x/(p_{0y}/p_{0x})$), carry on to the exit, compare with $p_b$, and iterate.
5. **Pipe after the nozzle? (Fanno)** Use $\frac{4fL}{D}=\left(\frac{4fL^*}{D}\right)_1-\left(\frac{4fL^*}{D}\right)_2$. If $L>L^*$ the flow chokes. **You can't subtract $L^*$ from $L$ when $L>L^*$** (Higgins's favourite trap).
6. **Heat added? (Rayleigh)** Use $q = c_p(T_{02}-T_{01})$. If $q > q^* = c_p(T_0^*-T_{01})$, the duct thermally chokes.

## The core equations (γ-general)

$$
\frac{T_0}{T}=1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2,\qquad \frac{p_0}{p}=\left(\frac{T_0}{T}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}},\qquad
\frac{A}{A^*}=\frac1M\left[\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\left(1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\right)\right]^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}
$$

$$
M_y^2=\frac{2+(\gamma-1)M_x^2}{2\gamma M_x^2-(\gamma-1)},\qquad \frac{p_y}{p_x}=\frac{2\gamma M_x^2-(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1},\qquad \frac{p_{0y}}{p_{0x}}=\frac{A^*_x}{A^*_y}
$$

$$
F = \dot m V_e + A_e(p_e-p_a),\qquad V_e=\sqrt{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}RT_0\left[1-\left(\frac{p_e}{p_0}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\right]}
$$

$$
M_{n1}=M_1\sin\sigma,\quad M_2=\frac{M_{n2}}{\sin(\sigma-\delta)},\qquad \nu_2=\nu_1+\theta,\qquad C_\pm:\ \nu\mp\theta=\text{const}
$$

$$
\frac{V_p'}{c_x}=\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\,\frac{M_s^2-1}{M_s},\qquad J_\pm=u\pm\frac{2c}{\gamma-1}\ \text{constant along}\ \frac{dx}{dt}=u\pm c
$$

## Five traps that cost the most marks

<Trap>

1. **Subtracting $L^*$ when $L > L^*$.** The flow can't be supersonic all the way. Look for the shock, or accept that the exit chokes.
2. **Forcing $p_e = p_b$ on a choked or supersonic exit.** Only a subsonic exit must match.
3. **Using $p_0$ from before the shock downstream of it.** $p_0$ and $A^*$ reset across the shock. $T_0$ doesn't.
4. **Entering Table A3 with the full Mach number on an oblique shock.** Use $M_n = M\sin\sigma$, and $M_2 = M_{n2}/\sin(\sigma-\delta)$.
5. **Wrong gas constant.** Always $R = 8314/MW$. Exams use MW = 28, 28.9, 30, 25 or 17.

</Trap>
