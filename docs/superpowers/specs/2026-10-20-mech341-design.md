# MECH 341 (Thermodynamics 2) — course design

Date: 2026-10-20 · Status: approved in chat (approach 1, real-world option C, mock finals kept)

## 1. Intent

Anthony (McGill Mech Eng graduate) took MECH 341 in Fall 2023 (Prof. Wagdi Habashi, Çengel/Boles/Kanoğlu 9th ed.). The site must pass the **guiding test**: *if he opens it in 5 years with nothing else, can he solve the midterm and the final?* — and, the priority for this course, **understand how the material is used in the real world**.

Standing rules (from the earlier courses, still binding):
- Detailed explanations: every step, why it is taken, its physical meaning, a numeric sanity check.
- Intuition plus real-life examples; realistic 3D hardware visuals (not flat boxes).
- Everything recomputed; errors in his notes, graded copies or printed answers get a ⚠ box with the corrected value and an entry on the errata page.
- Exercises come from **his** notes: take the exercise, compute the answer, check it matches his values; each exercise says what new thing it teaches.
- One-page summary + cheat sheets. Site is private: paraphrase copyrighted statements, never paste slide/textbook text or images.

## 2. Real-world framing (option C)

1. **Case-study spine.** Each chapter is built around one real machine, used from the opening to the "how it's examined" box, and the 3D labs are those machines.
2. **Per-problem real-world line.** Every worked problem ends its Think/Answer with a "Real world" sentence tying the numbers to real hardware (e.g. 9-109 *is* GE's 7FA; 14-108 is a power-plant cooling tower; 15-67 is a propane/natural-gas furnace).
3. **Real-world hub page** collecting the machines, their real numbers (efficiencies, pressure ratios, COPs, AFR, NOx limits) and links back.

Real numbers must be sourced or stated as typical/approximate; no invented precision.

`lib/` is the single physics source (already written: `props.ts`, `gas.ts`, `cycles.ts`, `data/*` generated by `scripts/gen-mech341-data.py`; `props.test.ts` passes). To add: `refrig.ts`, `psychro.ts`, `combustion.ts`, `equilibrium.ts`, `compressible.ts`, `realgas.ts` (Lee–Kesler simple fluid Z, Zh, Zs; Kay), `mixtures.ts`, and `lib.test.ts` with regression tests for every verified answer (DIGEST "VERIFICATION" section and the 2021 final).

Pages, calculators and labs take every number from `lib/`; worked-problem answers are covered by tests so the three cannot disagree. Textbook-table values shown on pages may differ slightly from `lib` (≤0.5 %); pages show the table value used in the solution and note the exact value when it matters.
